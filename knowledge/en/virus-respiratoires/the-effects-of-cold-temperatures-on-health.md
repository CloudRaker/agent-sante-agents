# The effects of cold temperatures on health

Official source: https://www.quebec.ca/en/health/advice-and-prevention/health-and-environment/the-effects-of-cold-temperatures-on-health

## Description

Official source: https://www.quebec.ca/en/health/advice-and-prevention/health-and-environment/the-effects-of-cold-temperatures-on-health

In Canada, cold weather is responsible for a significant number of health problems and deaths. It can have direct effects on health, such as [frostbite](https://www.quebec.ca/en/health/health-issues/a-z/frostbite) and [hypothermia](https://www.quebec.ca/en/health/health-issues/a-z/hypothermia). The cold can also have an indirect effect on health, by aggravating certain illnesses. Carbon monoxide poisoning is another indirect effect of the cold on health.

## Aggravation of pre-existing illnesses

Official source: https://www.quebec.ca/en/health/advice-and-prevention/health-and-environment/the-effects-of-cold-temperatures-on-health

Cold weather can aggravate certain respiratory illnesses, such as asthma, chronic bronchitis and emphysema especially when these are poorly controlled.

The health of people with cardiovascular illnesses can also worsen due to cold temperatures. People with angina or those who have already suffered a heart attack or a stroke can experience a worsening of symptoms.

## Carbon monoxide poisoning

Official source: https://www.quebec.ca/en/health/advice-and-prevention/health-and-environment/the-effects-of-cold-temperatures-on-health

During cold weather, some people use space heaters to keep warm at home. Also, during power outages, many people use appliances that burn fuel, such as propane, wood or oil. If they are not designed for indoor use or are not in good condition, these devices should not be used inside the house or in enclosed spaces (garage, shed, workshop, etc.) because they can release carbon monoxide. Be sure to place them at a safe distance from the house, away from air vents. Carbon monoxide detectors should be installed throughout the house.

Carbon monoxide is an invisible and odourless gas that can cause potentially fatal poisoning. For further information, consult the [Carbon monoxide poisoning](https://www.quebec.ca/en/health/health-issues/a-z/carbon-monoxide-poisoning) page.

## Protection and prevention

Official source: https://www.quebec.ca/en/health/advice-and-prevention/health-and-environment/the-effects-of-cold-temperatures-on-health

You can take certain precautions to prevent experiencing the effects of cold temperatures. You will find advice on how to protect yourself and your loved ones during periods of extreme cold on the [Preventing the harmful effects of cold temperatures](https://www.quebec.ca/en/health/advice-and-prevention/health-and-environment/preventing-the-harmful-effects-of-cold-temperatures) page.

## People at risk

Official source: https://www.quebec.ca/en/health/advice-and-prevention/health-and-environment/the-effects-of-cold-temperatures-on-health

Certain people are more at risk of suffering from the effects of extreme cold:

- Newborns and infants
- People aged 65 and over
- Wintersports enthusiasts
- People with reduced mobility
- People with reduced autonomy
- People with chronic illnesses, such as:
    - Cardiac or respiratory failure
    - Asthma
    - Diabetes
    - Malnutrition
    - Certain neurological disorders
- People with mental illness
- People living in poorly insulated houses
- Homeless people
- People who work outside

Some medications make people more sensitive to the cold. People who take medication for chronic illnesses or other diseases should seek information on the subject from a health-care professional.
