# Heat-Related Burn

Official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/heat-related-burn/

Heat Burn

## What it is

Official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/heat-related-burn/

A burn occurs when the skin comes into contact with something very hot, such as fire, steam, boiling liquid, or a hot surface. Water-filled blisters may also appear.

## What you can do at home

Official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/heat-related-burn/

What to do

- Cool the burn immediately by running cool (not ice-cold) water over it for at least 20 minutes.
- Remove clothing and jewelry near the burn, unless they are stuck to the skin.
- Gently wash the burn with tap water and mild, unscented soap.
- If the leg or arm is burned, keep that part of the body slightly elevated as often as possible for 1 to 2 days to help prevent swelling.
- To reduce pain, take acetaminophen (Tylenol) or ibuprofen (Advil). When in doubt, ask a pharmacist for advice.

Important: Ibuprofen is contraindicated for children under 6 months of age.

**If there is no open wound and the blister has not popped**

- Leave the burn uncovered.
- Apply an unscented moisturizer or aloe vera gel.
- Keep the burn clean.

**If there is an open wound, or if the blister has popped**

- Apply an antibiotic ointment (e.g., Polysporin), unless contraindicated. When in doubt, ask a pharmacist for advice.
- Cover the burn with a dressing and change it once a day or when it becomes dirty. Wash the burn before applying a new dressing.

What to avoid

- Do not apply butter, toothpaste, or other greasy products.
- Do not pop water blisters (also called blisters).

## When to seek medical attention

Official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/heat-related-burn/

A water blister (blister) larger than 2.5 cm

Pain that continues even after taking medication

A burn that has a bad smell, or redness, swelling, or warmth around the burn, or pus (yellow-green fluid)

Fever

A burn that has not healed after 14 days, even after following the recommended care

Difficulty breathing, coughing, a hoarse voice, or black mucus when coughing

It is important to check whether the burned person’s tetanus vaccination is up to date.

Need help?

Call Info-Santé at 811, Option 1, to speak with a nurse at any time.

Go to the pharmacy for advice on medications or treatment.

Make an appointment at your medical clinic or CLSC.

If you do not have a family doctor, go [gap.soinsvirtuels](https://gap.soinsvirtuels.gouv.qc.ca/en)  or call 811, Option 3.

Make an appointment through [Clic Santé](https://portal3.clicsante.ca/)  if a vaccination is needed.

## When to go to the hospital right away

Official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/heat-related-burn/

Clothing stuck to the burn

A burn caused by hot tar

A burn covering a large area of the body

A burn to the face, throat, or lungs

A burn that goes all the way around a limb, finger, neck, or chest

A severe burn on a joint, such as the knee, or on the hand, foot, or genitals
