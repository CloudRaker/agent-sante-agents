# Heat-Related Illness (when the body overheats)

Official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/heat-related-illness-when-body-overheats/

Heat exhaustion or Heat stroke

## What it is

Official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/heat-related-illness-when-body-overheats/

During extreme heat or intense physical activity in a hot environment, the body may have difficulty cooling itself. When the body becomes too hot, symptoms may include headache, nausea, diarrhea (loose, watery stools), or cramps in the legs, arms, or abdomen. Heat stroke is the most severe form of heat-related illness. It can develop quickly and become life-threatening if left untreated. Body temperature rises to a dangerously high level, and the person may become confused, disoriented, unconscious, or experience seizures (uncontrolled shaking of the body). It is important to go to the hospital immediately if you notice any of these symptoms.

## What you can do at home

Official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/heat-related-illness-when-body-overheats/

What to do

- Move to a cool place as soon as possible, lie down, and rest (in an air-conditioned, well-ventilated area, or in the shade).
- Apply cool, non-icy water to the entire body.
- Apply ice packs to the neck, armpits, and groin.
- If cramps occur, loosen clothing and gently massage the affected muscle.

Staying hydrated

- Drink cool water frequently in small amounts, unless advised otherwise by a healthcare professional.
- Use an electrolyte replacement drink from the pharmacy (such as Pedialyte or Gastrolyte), or make your own:
    - 360 mL unsweetened orange juice
    - 600 mL cooled boiled water
    - 2.5 mL (½ tsp) salt
- Drink lightly salted beverages, such as sports drinks (Gatorade, Powerade).

What to avoid

- Avoid intense physical activity for at least 12 hours.
- Do not take medication to reduce a fever.
- Do not apply alcohol to the skin.

Fever

Rectal temperature is:

- 38°C (100.4°F) or higher in babies younger than 3 months
- 40°C (104°F) or higher in babies 3 months of age and older, children, and adults

Unconsciousness (unresponsive

Seizures (uncontrolled shaking of the body)

Sudden change in overall condition, including:

- unusual fatigue (drowsiness) or difficulty staying awake
- limited responsiveness to surroundings
- confusion, agitation, or unusual behavior
- reduced appetite and decreased thirst
- difficulty carrying out usual daily activities

Rapid breathing

Excessive thirst and no urination for more than 12 hours

Symptoms that do not improve or that worsen despite following these recommendations
