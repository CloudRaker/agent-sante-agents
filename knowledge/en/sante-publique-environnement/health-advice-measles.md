# Measles

Official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/measles/

## What it is

Official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/measles/

Measles is a highly contagious illness caused by a virus. It spreads through the air when an infected person breathes, talks or coughs. Symptoms may include fever, a stuffy or runny nose, cough, a skin rash and red/irritated eyes. The eyes may also be watery and sensitive to light.

## What you can do at home

Official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/measles/

What to do

- Rest
- Drink water, herbal tea or broth
- For children: offer water, milk, juice or warm broth often
- Follow the recommendations provided by healthcare professionals.
- Before an appointment, tell the healthcare institution that you have measles. Precautions will be taken when you arrive.
- To relieve fever or pain, take ibuprofen (Advil). If in doubt, ask a pharmacist for advice.

Important: Ibuprofen is not recommended for children under 6 months of age.

**Watery eyes**

- Wash your hands before and after cleaning the eye
- Wipe the eye with a clean washcloth.
- Clean from the inner corner of the eye (near the nose) toward the outer corner.
- Make sure to use a different clean washcloth for each eye.
- Put the used washcloths in the laundry.
- Avoid rubbing the eyes.
- To relieve pain, place a cold or warm clean washcloth over the closed/irritated eye, depending on which feels better.
- Avoid bright light.
- Wear sunglasses if light is bothersome.

**To relieve other symptoms**

If needed, see the advice sheets for:

- [Cough](https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/cough/)
- [Stuffy nose](https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/stuffy-nose/) (nasal congestion)

**Limit the spread**

- Wash your hands often
- Do not share items that have come into contact with the infected person’s mouth.
- Stay home during the most contagious period: 4 days after the rash appear.
- Avoid close contact with others.
- Avoid contact with babies, children, older adults and people with weakened immune systems.
- Regularly clean and disinfect frequently touched surfaces and objects.

Avoid

- Smoking or vaping.
- Places where there is smoke or dust.

## When to seek medical attention

Official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/measles/

Ear pain that continues or gets worse, even after following the advice

Fever lasting more than 4 days after the skin rash appears

Fever that returns after 24 hours without a fever

Chest pain that does not go away or gets worse

Measles, mumps, rubella and chickenpox (varicella) vaccines not received or not up to date.

Make an appointment through [Clic Santé](https://portal3.clicsante.ca/services/measles-mumps-chickenpox-and-rubella-vaccination) if vaccination is needed.

Need help?

Visit the [Places and dates of possible exposure](https://www.quebec.ca/en/health/health-issues/a-z/measles/measles-outbreak#c265556) to a case of measles page to find out whether you may have been exposed to a case of measles.

Call Info-Santé at 811, Option 1, to speak with a nurse at any time.

Go to the pharmacy for advice on medications or treatment.

Make an appointment at your medical clinic or CLSC.

If you do not have a family doctor, go to [gap.soinsvirtuels](https://gap.soinsvirtuels.gouv.qc.ca/en) or call 811, Option 3.

## When to go to the hospital right away

Official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/measles/

Rapid changes in general condition:

- Unsual fatigue (drowsiness) or difficulty staying awake
- Reduced responsiveness to surroundings
- Confusion, agitation or unusual behaviour
- Hallucinations (seeing or hearing things that are not real)
- Reduced appetite or thirst

Difficulty breathing, especially with any of the following:

- Breathing that becomes increasingly difficult, even at rest
- A high-pitched or whistling sound when breathing (stridor)
- The chest or abdomen pulling inward with each breath (retractions)
- Blue or bluish skin, lips or fingers (cyanosis)
- In babies and children under the age of 3: nostrils flaring with each breath

Seizures (involuntary body movements)

A very severe headache with a stiff neck, vomiting or sensitivity to light
