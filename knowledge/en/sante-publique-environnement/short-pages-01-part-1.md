## Before a mosquito bites you... (https://publications.msss.gouv.qc.ca/msss/fichiers/2026/26-211-05WA.pdf)

Official source: https://publications.msss.gouv.qc.ca/msss/fichiers/2026/26-211-05WA.pdf

#### PROTECT YOURSELF!

Mosquitoes can transmit certain infections when they bite us. In Québec, the main infections that can be transmitted to humans are the West Nile virus (WNV) and the California serogroup viruses (CSV).

Although the infected persons show no symptoms in most cases, when symptoms are present, the most frequent are headaches and fever. In rare cases, neurological symptoms may occur, such as brain inflammation (encephalitis).

Anyone can contract WNV or CSV. People who work or spend a lot of time outdoors, and are more exposed to mosquitoes, are most at risk of getting infected.

Avoiding mosquito bites is the best way to prevent these infections.

##### Protective measures

Adopt simple personal protective measures to reduce your risks of being bitten.

- Wear long, light-coloured clothing, especially during periods when mosquitoes are active.

- Install screens in good condition on the doors and windows of your home, and on your tent and camping shelter.

- Cover playpens and strollers with mosquito nets when outside.

- Use mosquito repellent during your outdoor activities. It is recommended to use a mosquito repellent based on DEET, icaridin, lemon eucalyptus or soybean oil.

Try to reduce the number of mosquitoes in your environment, particularly by eliminating stagnant water around your residence.

You must also think about protection against mosquito bites when travelling, especially in tropical countries. In these cases, mosquitoes can transmit diseases such as malaria, Zika virus, dengue fever, Chikungunya virus and yellow fever virus.

If you have symptoms after a mosquito bite, call Info-Santé 811.

## Pubic Lice (Scabs) (https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/pubic-lice-scabs/)

Official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/pubic-lice-scabs/

Pediculosis pubis

### What it is

Pubic lice infestation (crabs) is the presence of lice (crabs) in pubic hair. They are mainly transmitted through intimate contact. They cause itching and can lead to redness on the skin.

Related sheet available: [Head Lice](https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/head-lice/)

### What you can do at home

What to do

- Use a pubic lice treatment product sold in pharmacies.
- Ask a pharmacist for advice on choosing the right product.
- Follow the product's instructions for use.
- Remove the nits (eggs) with a fine-tooth comb or tweezers.
- Wash your hands after applying the treatment.
- Change clothes and bedding after each treatment.
- Inform anyone with whom there has been intimate contact.
- Shaving pubic hair does not eliminate pubic lice.

**To prevent reinfestation**

- Cleaning the environment
- Clean clothing, bedding, and personal items used within the last 48 hours.
- Soak items in hot water for 5 to 10 minutes.
- Machine dry clothes on the hot cycle for 20 minutes.
- Store items that cannot be washed in a sealed plastic bag for 10 days.
- Vacuum mattresses.
- Clean the toilet seat.

If any lice treatment product is swallowed (ingested), immediately call the Centre antipoison du Québec at 1-800-463-5060.

Avoid

- Using a product designed for animals.
- Spraying an insecticide in the house. This measure is ineffective.

### When to seek medical attention

Lice or eggs (nits) still present after 2 treatments given 3 to 7 days apart

Irritated eyes after treating infested eyelashes

Itching that continues for a week after the last treatment, even after following instructions

Skin that becomes increasingly red or swollen due to scratching

When there is a need for being tested for sexually transmitted and blood-borne infections (STBBIs)

Need help?

Make an appointment through Clic Santé for [a screening](https://portal3.clicsante.ca/services/sti-screening).

Call Info-Santé at 811, Option 1, to speak with a nurse at any time.

Go to the pharmacy for advice on medications or treatment.

Make an appointment at your medical clinic or CLSC.

If you do not have a family doctor, go to [gap.soinsvirtuels](https://gap.soinsvirtuels.gouv.qc.ca/en) or call 811, Option 3.

### When to go to the hospital right away

Lice treatment product in the eyes with significant pain that continues after 30 minutes, even after rinsing the eye

## Sunburn (https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/sunburn/)

Official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/sunburn/

### What it is

A sunburn occurs when the skin becomes red and painful after too much time in the sun or in a tanning bed (excessive exposure to ultraviolet \[UV\] rays). The skin may hurt and feel like it is burning. Water blisters may also appear.

### What you can do at home

What to do

- Apply a cool, damp cloth to the skin or take a lukewarm bath for 10 to 20 minutes.
- Apply an unscented moisturizer or aloe vera gel.
- Drink plenty of fluids (e.g., water or an oral rehydration solution) to prevent dehydration.
- To reduce pain, take acetaminophen (Tylenol) or ibuprofen (Advil). When in doubt, ask a pharmacist for advice.

Important: Ibuprofen is contraindicated for children under 6 months of age.

**If there is an open wound or the blister has popped**

- Wash the burn.
- Apply an antibiotic ointment (e.g., Polysporin), unless contraindicated. Ask your pharmacist for advice if needed.
- Cover the burn with a dressing.
- Change the dressing once a day or when it becomes dirty.

What to avoid

- Do not pop water blisters.
- Do not apply ointments or products containing petroleum, cortisone, or pain-relieving creams.
- Avoid sun and UV exposure until the skin has healed.

### When to seek medical attention

The water blister is larger than 2.5 cm.

The pain continues even after taking medication.

The skin has a bad smell, or the skin around the sunburn becomes red, swollen, or warm, or there is pus (yellow-green fluid).

The skin does not heal, even after following the recommended care.

Need help?

Call Info-Santé at 811, Option 1, to speak with a nurse at any time.

Go to the pharmacy for advice on medications or treatment.

Make an appointment at your medical clinic or CLSC.

If you do not have a family doctor, go to [gap.soinsvirtuels](https://gap.soinsvirtuels.gouv.qc.ca/en) or call 811, Option 3.

### When to go to the hospital right away

Water blisters are present over a large area of the body.

You are unable to drink enough fluids to stay hydrated.

## Tuberculosis (https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/tuberculosis/)

Official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/tuberculosis/

Mycobacterium tuberculosis

### What it is

Tuberculosis is a bacterial infection spread through the air by coughing. The disease may develop shortly after contact with the bacteria or several years later. It mainly affects the lungs and may cause coughing, fever, blood in the phlegm, and night sweats.

### What you can do at home

What to do

- Rest.

**Medical follow-up and medication**

- Follow the healthcare professional’s recommendations.
- Attend all follow-up appointments.
- Take medication as prescribed.
- Complete the full course of medication, even if the person's condition improves.
- If medication causes side effects, ask a pharmacist for advice.

Avoid

- Éviter de fumer ou de vapoter.
- Éviter de consommer de l’alcool.

### When to seek medical attention

Difficulty taking the medication as prescribed or taking it incorrectly

Close contact with someone who has tuberculosis

If medication causes side effects, ask a pharmacist for advice

Need help?

Call Info-Santé at 811, Option 1, to speak with a nurse at any time.

Go to the pharmacy for advice on medications or treatment.

Make an appointment at your medical clinic or CLSC.

If you do not have a family doctor, go to [gap.soinsvirtuels](https://gap.soinsvirtuels.gouv.qc.ca/en) or call 811, Option 3.

### When to go to the hospital right away

Rapid changes in the person’s condition:

- unusual fatigue (drowsiness) or difficulty staying awake
- reduced responsiveness to what is happening around them
- confusion, agitation, or unusual behaviour
- hallucinations (seeing or hearing things that are not real)
- reduced appetite or thirst
- difficulty carrying out normal daily activities

## West Nile Virus (https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/west-nile-virus/)

Official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/west-nile-virus/

### What it is

West Nile virus is transmitted through the bite of an infected mosquito. Most people who become infected have no symptoms. When symptoms do occur, they often resemble those of a cold or the flu.

### What you can do at home

What to do

- Avoid going outdoors when mosquitoes are most active, around sunrise and sunset.
- Use insect repellent.
- Wear a hat, closed-toe shoes, and light-coloured, loose-fitting clothing that covers the arms and legs.
- Install screens on doors, windows, camping shelters, and strollers.
- Before travelling, check whether West Nile virus is being transmitted in the area being visited.
- Wear gloves when handling dead animals or birds.
- For babies and young children: Prevent any contact with dead animals or birds.

### When to go to the hospital right away

Rapid changes in general condition, such as:

- unusual fatigue or difficulty staying awake
- reduced responsiveness
- confusion, agitation or unusual behaviour
- hallucinations (seeing or hearing things that are not real)
- reduced appetite or thirst
- difficulty carrying out normal daily activities

Seizures (involuntary body movements)

Severe headache with a stiff neck (pain at the back of the neck), vomiting, or fever

Difficulty breathing that does not improve or gets worse

Grande faiblesse musculaire ou incapacité à bouger Severe muscle weakness or inability to move part of the body (paralysis)partie du corps (paralysie)

Need help?

Call Info-Santé at 811, Option 1, to speak with a nurse at any time.

Go to the pharmacy for advice on medications or treatment.

Make an appointment at your medical clinic or CLSC.

If you do not have a family doctor, go to [gap.soinsvirtuels](https://gap.soinsvirtuels.gouv.qc.ca/en) or call 811, Option 3.

## Japanese encephalitis (https://www.quebec.ca/en/health/health-issues/a-z/japanese-encephalitis)

Official source: https://www.quebec.ca/en/health/health-issues/a-z/japanese-encephalitis

### Transmission

Japanese encephalitis is spread by the bite of a mosquito infected by the Japanese encephalitis virus.

### Signs and symptoms

- Fever
- Headache
- Drowsiness (sleepiness), disorientation, tremors, and sometimes seizures

Only 1 infected person in 200 has symptoms.

### Possible complications

- Permanent brain damage such as paralysis, epilepsy, and mental retardation (33-50% of infected persons)
- Death (10-25% of cases)

### Available vaccine

- [Japanese encephalitis vaccine](https://www.quebec.ca/en/health/advice-and-prevention/vaccination/japanese-encephalitis-vaccine)

Last update: October 22, 2015

## Expiry dates (https://www.quebec.ca/en/health/nutrition/food-safety-risk-prevention/food-safety/buying-food/expiry-dates)

Official source: https://www.quebec.ca/en/health/nutrition/food-safety-risk-prevention/food-safety/buying-food/expiry-dates

When you purchase prepackaged food, always check its expiry date or best before date. This date indicates the end of the food’s shelf life. Quality may decline once that date has passed.

The expiry or best before date also indicates how fresh a packaged food item is, as long as it has been stored properly.

Manufacturers and distributors must label most prepackaged foods with their storage life if it is 90 days or less.

### Exceptions

**Fresh fruits and vegetables:** Retailers are not required to specify a storage life.

**Food packaged at the point of sale:** Retailers can post a storage life near the food instead of on the label. You will see this sometimes in the fresh meat aisle.

**Products packaged on demand:** Products packaged by a clerk at a consumer’s request do not need a label (e.g., deli meat and cheese sliced and packaged on demand, boxed lunches or takeout meals purchased at the counter).

## Indication of the origin of Québec fruits and vegetables (https://www.quebec.ca/en/health/nutrition/food-safety-risk-prevention/food-safety/buying-food/indication-origin-fruits-vegetables)

Official source: https://www.quebec.ca/en/health/nutrition/food-safety-risk-prevention/food-safety/buying-food/indication-origin-fruits-vegetables

Consumers want to know the precise origins of the fruits and vegetables they purchase, for various reasons:

- They are looking for high-quality fresh fruits and vegetables.
- They want to support certain producers.
- They want to buy local.
- They want to avoid supporting certain producers or countries by purchasing their products (for political, humanitarian or other reasons).

### Retailers’ obligations

Fresh fruits and vegetables grown in Québec must be traceable at the retail point of sale. Retailers must indicate that these agricultural products were grown in Québec:

- When they are prepackaged or sold in bulk at retail, such as at markets, fruit stands and grocery stores
- In all advertising for their sale, including flyers and posters

To avoid confusion, the authorized expressions are:

- Product of Québec
- Grown in Québec
- Harvested in Québec
- Name of the product, followed by “of Québec” (e.g., strawberries of Québec)

## Reusable bags and containers (https://www.quebec.ca/en/health/nutrition/food-safety-risk-prevention/food-safety/buying-food/reusable-bags-containers)

Official source: https://www.quebec.ca/en/health/nutrition/food-safety-risk-prevention/food-safety/buying-food/reusable-bags-containers

Many restaurants and grocery stores allow you to bring your own reusable bags and containers to buy food in bulk or bring your meal home.

Ask your grocer or restaurateur if they accept consumers’ personal containers. If they do, both of you must approach hygiene carefully.

- Consumers must bring clean containers.
- Operators must refuse to serve food in dirty containers. If they cannot monitor the cleanliness of the containers, they should not allow this practice in their establishment.
- Operators must use hygienic work processes to prevent the risk of contamination. For example, consumers’ containers must not be placed on work surfaces that come in direct contact with food.
- Staff who touch consumers’ containers should wash their hands often.
- Operators who guarantee that the food they prepare or serve is allergen-free cannot accept customers’ containers.

If food poisoning occurs, any resulting investigations will take the use of consumers’ containers into account.

### Cleaning grocery bags

Reusable bags must be cleaned often to avoid cross-contamination.

- Use a clean sponge or cloth to wash the bags with hot, soapy water.
- Dry the bags inside out, or wipe the inside of the bag dry using a clean, dry cloth.
- Fabric bags can easily be washed in the washing machine. Read the label for care instructions.
