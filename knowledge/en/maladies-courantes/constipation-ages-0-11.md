# Constipation – Ages 0-11

Official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/constipation-ages-0-11/

## What it is

Official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/constipation-ages-0-11/

Constipation is when it is hard to have a bowel movement (poop) or when bowel movements happen less often than usual. Stools (poop) may be hard. There may be stomach pain or a feeling of still needing to go, even after a bowel movement. Constipation is not a cause for concern, but it is important to find a way to get relief.

Related sheet available: [Constipation – Ages 12 and over](https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/constipation-ages-12-and-over/)

## What you can do at home

Official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/constipation-ages-0-11/

What to do

**For babies under 6 months**

- Do not give water or any liquids other than breast milk or infant formula.
- Make sure the baby drinks enough milk for their age.

**If the baby is formula-fed:**

- Follow the preparation instructions on the formula package.
- Choose a formula best suited to the baby’s age.
- For babies over 6 months who eat solid foods
- Offer water every day, in addition to milk.
- Offer high-fiber foods, such as:
    - vegetables;
    - fruit (prunes, pears, and apples);
    - whole-grain breads and cereals;
    - legumes.

**Children ages 1 to 11**

- Offer high-fiber foods at least 3 times a day, such as fruit (bananas, pears, figs, plums), vegetables, whole-grain breads and cereals, and legumes.
- Add 1 teaspoon of wheat bran to cereal or yogurt.
- Serve small meals.
- Encourage the child to chew food thoroughly.
- Offer mainly water to drink.
- Offer apple, pear, or prune juice.

Important: Some foods, such as dried fruit, can be a choking hazard depending on the child’s age. Cut them into small pieces or prepare them so they are safe for the child to eat.

**Making bowel movements easier**

During toilet training:

- Offer a potty rather than the toilet.
- Every day, encourage the child to sit on the potty at the same times for 5 to 10 minutes, especially after meals (toileting schedule).
- Let the child watch a parent use the toilet.
- Comfort the child if painful bowel movements are making toilet training harder.
- If needed, take a temporary break from toilet training.

If the child is toilet trained:

- Every day, encourage the child to sit on the toilet at the same times for 5 to 10 minutes, especially after meals (toileting schedule).
- Provide a small stool for the child to rest their feet on.
- Gently massage the abdomen in a clockwise direction 3 to 4 times a day.
- Encourage the child to be active during playtime.

Avoid

- Do not use medications to help with bowel movements (laxatives) without medical advice.
- Ask a pharmacist for advice before using any constipation treatment.

## When to seek medical attention

Official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/constipation-ages-0-11/

Constipation that keeps coming back

Difficulty passing stools

For babies under 1 year:

- fever and no bowel movements
- stools that are often small, watery, or hard
- fewer than 2 bowel movements a day in breastfed babies under 4 weeks old

For babies and children under 11:

- constipation (no bowel movements) for several days
- frequent pain when having a bowel movement
- ribbon-shaped stools

For children ages 1 to 11:

- red blood in the stool (even a small amount)
- refusing to eat
- not gaining enough weight

For children ages 4 and over: passing stool into their underwear without meaning to (involuntary leakage)

Need help?

Call Info-Santé at 811, Option 1, to speak with a nurse at any time.

Go to the pharmacy for advice on medications or treatment.

Make an appointment at your medical clinic or CLSC.

If you do not have a family doctor, go to [gap.soinsvirtuels](https://gap.soinsvirtuels.gouv.qc.ca/en) or call 811, Option 3.

## When to go to the hospital right away

Official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/constipation-ages-0-11/

Black blood or a lot of red blood in the stool

Frequent vomiting, or vomit that is green or yellow-green

Rapid changes in overall condition:

- unusual tiredness (drowsiness) or difficulty staying awake
- less responsive to what is happening around them
- confusion, agitation, or unusual behaviour
- hallucinations (seeing or hearing things that are not real)
- reduced appetite or thirst
- difficulty carrying out usual daily activities

For babies under 3 months of age: fever

For babies under 1 year:

- stomach that is very painful to the touch
- continuous crying that cannot be soothed
- swollen stomach with frequent vomiting

For children ages 1 to 11: stomach that is very hard and very painful for more than 2 hours
