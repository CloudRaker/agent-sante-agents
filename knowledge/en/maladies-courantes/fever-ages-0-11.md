# Fever – Ages 0-11

Official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/fever-ages-0-11/

## What is it?

Official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/fever-ages-0-11/

A fever is when the body temperature is higher than normal. It often happens when someone is sick or when the body is fighting an infection.

Related sheet available: [Fever – Ages 12 and over](https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/fever-ages-12-and-over/)

## A child has a fever when their temperature reaches:

Official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/fever-ages-0-11/

| Method | Ages 0-11 |
| --- | --- |
| Taken rectally (anus) | 38 °C (100.4 °F) or higher |
| Under the armpit | 37.5 °C (99.5 °F) or higher |
| By mouth | 37.5 °C (99.5 °F) or higher |

For children younger than 5 years, it is recommended to take the temperature rectally (anus).

For children 5 years and older, it is recommended to take the temperature orally.

Temperature can vary depending on the method and thermometer used. Take the temperature the same way each time to better track any changes.

## What you can do at home

Official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/fever-ages-0-11/

What to do

- Let the child rest to help the body heal.
- Offer fluids frequently, unless a healthcare professional advises otherwise.
- Offer food according to the child’s appetite and what the child can eat.
- Keep the temperature in the home comfortable.
- Adjust blankets or clothing as needed to prevent the child from becoming too hot, too cold, or having chills.
- Keep track of symptoms and temperature. This information may be helpful when calling 811 or during a medical consultation.

**To relieve fever**

- Give acetaminophen (Tylenol) or ibuprofen (Advil, Motrin).
- Acetaminophen and ibuprofen can be given alternately, while following the recommended dosing interval.
- When in doubt, ask a pharmacist for advice.

Important: Ibuprofen is contraindicated for children under 6 months of age.

What to avoid

- Rubbing alcohol on the skin.
- Applying ice.
- Cold showers or baths.

## When to seek medical attention

Official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/fever-ages-0-11/

The fever lasts for more than 72 hours (3 days) despite medication.

The fever returns after being gone for 24 hours.

A return within the past 12 weeks from a country where there are health risks, according to the

[Government of Canada](https://travel.gc.ca/).

Need help?

Call Info-Santé at 811, Option 1, to speak with a nurse at any time.

Go to the pharmacy for advice on medications or treatment.

Make an appointment at your medical clinic or CLSC.

If you do not have a family doctor, go to [gap.soinsvirtuels](https://gap.soinsvirtuels.gouv.qc.ca/en) or call 811, Option 3.

## When to go to the hospital right away

Official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/fever-ages-0-11/

Unusual fatigue, difficulty staying awake or waking up, or excessive sleepiness

Little or no response to what is happening around the child

Confusion, agitation, or behavior that is unusual for the child

Fever accompanied by back or rib pain, nausea, or vomiting

Constant crying; the child cannot be consoled

Eating or drinking less, with difficulty carrying out usual activities

Stiff neck or neck pain

Seizures

Signs of dehydration:

- Very dry mouth.
- Little or no urine (pee) despite drinking plenty of fluids.

Difficulty breathing, especially if:

- The nostrils flare when the child breathes.
- The lips or fingers are blue or bluish (cyanosis).
- The child makes a high-pitched sound when breathing (stridor).
- The stomach or chest pulls inward with each breath (retractions).

After receiving a vaccine

- Rectal (anus) temperature above 38 °C (100.4 °F) for more than 4 hours.
- Rectal (anus) temperature above 40 °C (104 °F).

For babies under 3 months

- Underarm temperature of 37.5 °C (99.5 °F) or higher.
- Rectal temperature (anus) of 38 °C (100.4 °F) or higher.
- Rectal temperature (anus) of 36 °C (96.8 °F) or lower.
