## 8 – LIST OF SYMPTOMS

Official source: https://publications.msss.gouv.qc.ca/msss/fichiers/2025/25-902-06A.pdf

Treatments you receive are intended to treat the disease, but they may sometimes result in the side effects listed below. However, your healthcare team may be able to suggest ways to relieve side effects, thereby improving your quality of life. The following is a general list of symptoms that may arise depending on the treatment you receive. If you have one or more of them, it is important that you notify your contact people as soon as possible. During the day, contact your professional support team whose telephone numbers are at the beginning of the passport. During the evening or at night, call **Info-Santé 811** or any other emergency number given to you by the treatment team. However, don’t hesitate to call if you are concerned about any other symptoms or situations that may arise.

**Please note that this table is only a guide. Remember that you can best assess what you are feeling. Trust your feelings. Do not stay at home and allow a situation to worsen and potentially lead to complications and/or delay your treatment. If you have any of these symptoms but are unable to reach a healthcare provider to assess your physical condition, you must go to the emergency room.**

## 9 – MONITORING SYMPTOMS AT HOME

Official source: https://publications.msss.gouv.qc.ca/msss/fichiers/2025/25-902-06A.pdf

If you monitor your symptoms at home, this scale from 0 to 10 can help you to determine and record the levels of severity and keep track of the methods you use to relieve them.

You should have this information on hand when going to your clinic or telephoning for assistance, since attending medical staff could find it of help in identifying the best steps for relieving your symptoms.

The following example uses pain as an example. The scale can be applied to any symptom you experience.

0<br>

10<br>1 2 3 4 5 6 7 8 9<br>

**0 = No symptom 10 = Unbearable symptom**

Example for the table below: **If you have no pain, enter 0** , in the “pain” column. **If you experience unbearable pain, write down 10.**

Do the same for the other symptoms, writing down the date of your assessment in the left-hand column.

In the appropriate cell and based on the scale provided above, write down the degree at which you perceive each symptom.

Your local or hospital pharmacy may be able to print out a list of drugs you have been prescribed. **Ask for a printout and keep it with your passport.**

Before your treatments, **you should tell your oncologist and oncology pharmacist about any nonfood substance** (natural products, vitamins, and others) you consume, even occasionally. Certain substances can interact with your chemotherapy drugs either by reducing the effectiveness of the treatment or increasing treatment side effects.

## THE QUEBEC CANCER FOUNDATION

Official source: https://publications.msss.gouv.qc.ca/msss/fichiers/2025/25-902-06A.pdf

The Quebec Cancer Foundation (QCF) offers resources, programs and services to people who have cancer and their loved ones, such as:

- a documentation centre that lists and keeps up to date multiple print and electronic materials throughout Quebec. A free mail service is also available;

- an Info cancer Hotline where professionals, including oncology nurses, answer questions and provide information;

- a cancer information web portal;

- various in-person and online complementary therapy programs;

- a telephone peer matching service to talk to someone who has had a similar experience;

- an accommodation network in Montreal, Quebec City, Sherbrooke, Gatineau, Trois-Rivières and Lévis;

- a directory of 2 200 resources available across the province.

- **A single number: 1 800 363 0063 | Website: cancerquebec.ca/en**

## THE CANADIAN CANCER SOCIETY

Official source: https://publications.msss.gouv.qc.ca/msss/fichiers/2025/25-902-06A.pdf

The Canadian Cancer Society (CCS) offers many services to support and inform people who have cancer and their loved ones, such as:

- a cancer information helpline, accessible by telephone, email and live chat, staffed by specialists with scientific training. They can provide you with quality information as well as practical resources depending on your needs;

- a peer support community, **CancerConnection.ca** , where people who have cancer and their loved ones can share their experiences;

- financial assistance for transportation, the loan of wigs and the donation of temporary breast prostheses;

- a lodge located in Montreal;

- easy-to-understand pamphlets about cancer and cancer treatments;

- a directory of services available in your community;

- a documented website containing, among other things, electronic versions of the pamphlets and an extensive medical encyclopedia.

**A single number: 1 888 939 3333 | Website: cancer.ca/en**

## LEUCAN

Official source: https://publications.msss.gouv.qc.ca/msss/fichiers/2025/25-902-06A.pdf

Leucan’s mission is to promote the recovery and well-being of children who have cancer and their families through accompaniment, support and research support at all stages of the disease, and even afterwards.

While supporting clinical research and the four pediatric oncology centres in Quebec, Leucan accompanies the children and their families at all stages of the disease and, when necessary, in the bereavement process through its many services and programs, such as:

- an intake, support and accompaniment service;

- financial assistance;

- a massage therapy program;

- a guided activities service in most pediatric oncology centres in Quebec;

- social and recreational activities;

- a school awareness program;

- activities for young people aged 12 to 17;

- a bereavement follow-up program.

**A single number: 1 800 361 9643 | Website: leucan.qc.ca/en**

## RESOURCES TO HELP YOU QUIT SMOKING OR VAPING

Official source: https://publications.msss.gouv.qc.ca/msss/fichiers/2025/25-902-06A.pdf

Quitting smoking is one of the best things you can do to improve your health and quality of life. Take advantage of I QUIT NOW services and the expertise of quit smoking and vaping specialists. It is effective, free, non judgmental and available in French and English throughout Quebec.

Visit the I QUIT NOW website (tobaccofreequebec.ca/iquitnow) for help and information on these services:

- **in-person or virtual help** : contact a Quit Smoking centre now for individual or group meetings (sante.gouv.qc.ca/repertoire ressources);

- **helpline** : at any time, talk to a specialist on the I QUIT NOW telephone line at 1 866 527 7383 (1 866 527-7383);

- **online help** : sign up for free I QUIT NOW online help to find out about support tools and videos.

Doctors, pharmacists, respiratory therapists and nurses can also advise you and prescribe nicotine replacement therapies and medications to help you quit smoking.
