## Carbohydrate counting guide for people living with diabetes - Simplified method (https://publications.msss.gouv.qc.ca/msss/fichiers/2024/24-215-02A_Appendix.pdf)

Official source: https://publications.msss.gouv.qc.ca/msss/fichiers/2024/24-215-02A_Appendix.pdf

**Carbohydrate counting guide for people living with diabetes** Appendix – For health professionals

### Appendix – For health professionals
This appendix explains the criteria that were used to create the _Carbohydrate counting guide for people living with diabetes_ .

### Nutritional values
For foods that do not have a nutrition facts table, most of the values come from the _Canadian Nutrient File_ (CNF).

For other foods, the values for a few store-bought products and the CNF values were compiled to obtain averages.

### Serving sizes
For foods with carbohydrates, each serving contains 15 g +/- 3 g of carbohydrates.

For foods with little or no carbohydrates, each serving contains 5 g of carbohydrates + / - 1 g of carbohydrates. If no serving is indicated, the typical amount eaten contains less than 5 g of carbohydrates.

If “Varies” is indicated for a serving, it means that a serving size could not be determined using the above-mentioned criteria.

### Symbols
Foods that are sources of fibre and foods that are high in saturated fat, unsaturated fat and sodium are identified by symbols.

The criteria used are **drawn** from Health Canada’s nutrition labelling regulations for packaged foods.

- ` All fruits, vegetables, whole grains and whole grain products have been identified as sources of fibre even if they did not meet the criteria. For other store-bought foods, Health Canada’s criteria were used.

- ` For saturated fats, Health Canada’s criteria were used.

- ` For unsaturated fats, there are no official recommendations. The symbol has been applied to foods that are known to be nutritious and high in unsaturated fat.

- ` For sodium, the threshold used (2,000 mg) is lower than Health Canada’s threshold (2,300 mg) given that people living with diabetes are at high risk of developing cardiovascular disease.

- ` For each store-bought food, several brands were analyzed. If a brand met the criteria for sodium or saturated fat, the food was identified with the appropriate symbol.

### Other foods
For canned fruit, the servings indicated take the juice or syrup into account.

Exceptions were made based on the editorial board’s judgement.

## Allergy (https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/allergy/)

Official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/allergy/

### What it is

It happens when the body overreacts to something that is normally harmless, such as peanuts, pollen, cats, or eggs. This reaction can cause symptoms like sneezing, itchy skin, or a runny nose.

### What you can do at home

What to do

- For allergies that have already been assessed by a healthcare professional, follow the recommendations provided.
- Monitor symptoms, especially during the first 2 hours after exposure to an allergen.
- Ask a pharmacist whether over-the-counter medications may help.

**If symptoms appear quickly after contact with something that has previously caused a severe allergic reaction**

- Take epinephrine (e.g., EpiPen, Allerject, or Emerade) immediately.
- If epinephrine is not available, call 911 immediately.

**If symptoms do not appear immediately after contact with something that has previously caused a severe allergic reaction**

- Go to the emergency department immediately.
- Keep epinephrine (e.g., EpiPen, Allerject, or Emerade) close at hand during transport.
- Take epinephrine immediately if symptoms appear and worsen rapidly.

### When to seek medical attention

Unexplained difficulty breathing

Frequent hives or skin redness

Persistent runny nose or red eyes

Recurrent nausea, vomiting, or diarrhea

Need help?

Call Info-Santé at 811 (Option 1) to speak with a nurse at any time.

Go to the pharmacy for advice on medications or treatment.

Make an appointment at your medical clinic or CLSC.

If you do not have a family doctor, go to [gap.soinsvirtuels](https://gap.soinsvirtuels.gouv.qc.ca/en) or call 811, option 3.

### When to go to the hospital right away

After receiving epinephrine (e.g., EpiPen, Allerject, or Emerade), even if you are feeling better.

After being in contact with something that has previously caused a severe allergic reaction.

Symptoms of a severe allergic reaction (redness, itchy skin, swelling of the lips, tongue, or face), along with at least one of the following:

- High-pitched breathing sounds (stridor) or difficulty breathing.
- Bluish or purplish lips or fingers.
- Feeling weak or dizzy, or loss of consciousness.
- Very fast heartbeat.
- Stomach pain, vomiting, or diarrhea.

For young children (0–2 years):

not behaving as usual, being limp, or being difficult to wake up.

## Anaphylactic Shock (https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/anaphylactic-shock/)

Official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/anaphylactic-shock/

Anaphylaxis

### What it is

Anaphylaxis is a very severe allergic reaction. It often occurs quickly, within minutes, but can sometimes occur up to 2 hours after touching or eating something that causes the allergy (an allergen).

Related sheets available: [Allergy](https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/allergy/), [Food Allergies or Intolerances](https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/food-allergies-or-intolerances/)

### Signs of anaphylaxis

Symptoms of a severe allergic reaction (redness, itchy skin, or swelling of the lips, tongue or face), along with at least one of the following:
\- A high-pitched sound when breathing (stridor) or difficulty breathing.
\- Bluish or purplish lips or fingers.
\- Feeling weak or dizzy, or loss of consciousness.
\- A very rapid heartbeat.
\- Stomach ache, vomiting or diarrhea (watery stools).

### What you can do at home

What to do

If it is a severe allergic reaction, call 911 immediately.

- Inject epinephrine (e.g., EpiPen, Allerject or Emerade) as soon as possible, if available.
- Do not drive yourself to the emergency department. Ask someone else to drive.

**How to use epinephrine** (e.g., EpiPen, Allerject or Emerade)

- Follow the instructions provided by the pharmacist or those included with the epinephrine.
- Give a second dose of epinephrine after 10 minutes if symptoms continue.

**While waiting for emergency services**

- Loosen tight clothing.
- Lie down and raise your legs.
- Sit up if you have difficulty breathing.
- Lie on your side if vomiting occurs.
- Avoid sudden movements.
- For young children: help them stay calm and reassure them.

For pregnant people: lie on the left side.

### When to go to the hospital right away

After receiving epinephrine (e.g., EpiPen, Allerject or Emerade):

- even if symptoms improve or go away
- even if the injection was given by accident

After contact with a food or substance that has previously caused a severe allergic reaction

Need help?

Call Info-Santé at 811, Option 1, to speak with a nurse at any time.

Go to the pharmacy for advice on medications or treatment.

Make an appointment at your medical clinic or CLSC.

If you do not have a family doctor, go to [gap.soinsvirtuels](https://gap.soinsvirtuels.gouv.qc.ca/en) or call 811, Option 3.

## Cut (https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/cut/)

Official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/cut/

Laceration

### What it is

A laceration, or cut, is an open wound caused by a cut or tear in the skin. It can happen after a fall, a bump, or contact with a sharp object. The wound may bleed and sometimes hurt.

### What you can do at home

What to do

- Wash your hand with soap and water before and after touching the cut.
- If the cut is on a finger, hand, foot, or toe, remove any rings or bracelets.
- If the cut is bleeding, apply pressure for a few minutes with a clean cloth or bandage.
- If the cut is bleeding heavily or the injured person feels their heartbeat in the wound, raise the injured arm or leg above the level of the heart.
- Once the bleeding has slowed, gently clean the wound with tap water and mild fragrance-free soap for a few minutes.
- Once the bleeding has stopped, cover the cut with a bandage. Change the bandage once a day or whenever it becomes dirty.
- To help relieve pain, take acetaminophen (Tylenol) or ibuprofen (Advil). When in doubt, ask a pharmacist for advice.

Important: Ibuprofen is contraindicated for children under 6 months of age.

### When to seek medical attention

The cut is large or deep.

An object is stuck in the wound.

The cut continues to bleed, even with pressure.

The pain continues despite taking medication.

The wound has a bad smell.

The skin around the cut becomes red, swollen, or warm, or there is pus (yellow-green fluid).

The injured person has a fever.

It is important to check whether the injured person’s tetanus vaccination is up to date.

Need help?

Call Info-Santé at 811, Option 1, to speak with a nurse at any time.

Go to the pharmacy for advice on medications or treatment.

Make an appointment on [Clic Santé](https://portal3.clicsante.ca/) if your child needs a vaccination.

Make an appointment at your medical clinic or CLSC.

If you do not have a family doctor, go to [gap.soinsvirtuels](https://gap.soinsvirtuels.gouv.qc.ca/en) or call 811, Option 3.

### When to go to the hospital right away

Blood is spurting from the cut.

The injured finger, hand, or body part cannot be moved.

The injured person has numbness, weakness, or loss of sensation around the wound.

## Fatigue (https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/fatigue/)

Official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/fatigue/

Weakness

### What it is

Fatigue is a feeling of low energy that can make everyday activities more difficult. It may be harder to stay active or concentrate. Fatigue can be caused by illness, medical treatment, lack of sleep, stress, or a busy lifestyle.

### What you can do at home

What to do

- Rest between activities.
- Plan activities for the times of day when you have the most energy.
- Stop and rest before becoming overly tired.
- Organize your space to reduce unnecessary effort and movement.

**To help you rest**

- Keep your surroundings quiet, comfortable, and at a suitable temperature.
- Avoid noise and other distractions during rest periods.
- Maintain a regular sleep routine.
- Choose relaxing activities such as reading, listening to calming music, or taking a warm bath.

**Healthy lifestyle habits**

- Eat regularly throughout the day.
- Drink enough water.
- Do light to moderate physical activity, preferably in the morning or early afternoon.

### When to seek medical attention

Persistent sadness, irritability, or loss of interest in usual activities

Frequent dizziness or problems with balance

Fatigue that continues despite rest or after following the recommended advice

Rapid weight loss with no clear cause

Ongoing difficulty sleeping, despite efforts to improve sleep habits

Difficulty carrying out everyday activities

For children and teenagers:

difficulty playing, concentrating, participating in school, or following usual routines

Need help?

Call Info-Santé at 811, Option 1, to speak with a nurse at any time.

Go to the pharmacy for advice on medications or treatment.

Make an appointment at your medical clinic or CLSC.

If you do not have a family doctor, go [gap.soinsvirtuels](https://gap.soinsvirtuels.gouv.qc.ca/en) or call 811, Option 3.

### When to go to the hospital right away

Sudden weakness in an arm, leg, or one side of the body

Severe chest pain

Difficulty breathing that does not improve, especially if:

- breathing becomes increasingly difficult, even at rest
- there is a high-pitched breathing sound (stridor)
- the chest or abdomen pulls inward with each breath (retractions)

Unexplained loss of consciousness

Sudden severe weakness or extreme fatigue that is much worse than usual

## Impetigo (https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/impetigo/)

Official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/impetigo/

### What it is

Impetigo is a bacterial skin infection that causes clusters of small red sores, often covered with a yellow crust. Impetigo spreads easily, especially in childcare settings, and is more common in warm, humid weather.

### What you can do at home

What to do

- Soak the sores in soapy water at least twice a day.
- Remove any crusts that come off easily. Do not pull off crusts that are still attached to the sore.
- Avoid scratching.

**If an antibiotic cream is prescribed**

- Follow the healthcare professional’s instructions.
- Apply the antibiotic cream to the sores.
- Cover the sores with a dry bandage until they have dried.

**To prevent spreading the infection**

- Wash hands often.
- Do not share towels or washcloths.
- Regularly clean and disinfect frequently touched surfaces and objects.
- Cough and sneeze into the elbow or a tissue.

### When to seek medical attention

The sores get larger 48 hours after treatment begins

Red streaks extend from the sores

A blister or sore is more than 2.5 cm wide

The skin around a sore becomes painful, red, swollen, or warm, or contains pus (yellow-green fluid)

The affected person has eczema (atopic dermatitis) or is immunocompromised

For children under 1 year of age:

consulter si des symptômes apparaissent après un contact avec une personne atteinte

Need help?

Call Info-Santé at 811, Option 1, to speak with a nurse at any time.

Go to the pharmacy for advice on medications or treatment.

Make an appointment at your medical clinic or CLSC.

If you do not have a family doctor, go to [gap.soinsvirtuels](https://gap.soinsvirtuels.gouv.qc.ca/en) or call 811, Option 3.

### When to go to the hospital right away

Red streaks extending from the sores, along with:

- signs of an infection that is getting worse quickly such as skin that becomes red, swollen, or very painful
- fever, chills or shaking
- rapid heartbeat, or palpitations

Rapid changes in general condition, such as:

- unusual fatigue or difficulty staying awake
- reduced responsiveness
- confusion, agitation or unusual behaviour
- hallucinations (seeing or hearing things that are not real)
- reduced appetite or thirst
- difficulty carrying out normal daily activities

## Indoor allergen allergy (https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/indoor-allergen-allergy/)

Official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/indoor-allergen-allergy/

Allergy to Mold, Dust Mites or Animals

### What it is

This is an allergy to substances commonly found in the home, such as mold, dust mites or animal dander. It occurs when the body reacts to the particles in the air that we breathe or come into contact with.

Related sheets available: [Allergy](https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/allergy/), [Difficulty Breathing](https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/difficulty-breathing/)

### What you can do at home

What to do

- Vacuum furniture and rugs.
- Dust often with a damp cloth, especially in bedrooms.
- Choose curtains and rugs that are easy to wash.
- Use dust mite covers on mattresses and pillows.
- Wash bedding (sheets and blankets) in hot water every week.
- Put items that cannot be washed in the dryer on a hot setting.
- If you are allergic to an animal, consider no longer living with the animal.

**For young children**

- Keep only a few stuffed animals or dolls in their bedroom.
- Wash stuffed animals and dolls in hot water at least twice a month.

Avoid

- Reduce clutter and other items that collect dust, such as trinkets, upholstered furniture, rugs and carpeting.
- Avoid feather pillows, thick down comforters, flannel sheets and wool blankets.
- Avoid shaking out bedding.
- Avoid smoke and anything else that can irritate the lungs, such as dust or chemicals.
- Avoid using floor or ceiling fans, especially in bedrooms.

### When to seek medical attention

Difficulty breathing for no apparent reason

A skin rash (redness or bumps) for no apparent reason

Frequent red eyes or a runny nose for no apparent reason

Need help?

Call Info-Santé at 811, Option 1, to speak with a nurse at any time.

Go to the pharmacy for advice on medications or treatment.

Make an appointment at your medical clinic or CLSC.

If you do not have a family doctor, go to [gap.soinsvirtuels](https://gap.soinsvirtuels.gouv.qc.ca/en) or call 811, Option 3.

### When to go to the hospital right away

Symptoms of a severe allergic reaction (redness, itchy skin, or swelling of the lips, tongue or face), along with at least one of the following:

- A high-pitched sound when breathing (stridor) or difficulty breathing
- Bluish or purplish lips or fingers
- Feeling weak or dizzy, or loss of consciousness
- A very rapid heartbeat
- Stomach ache, vomiting or diarrhea (watery stools)

For young children (ages 0 to 2):

not responding as usual, being limp or difficult to wake

## Low Blood Pressure (https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/low-blood-pressure/)

Official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/low-blood-pressure/

Arterial hypotension

### What it is

Low blood pressure is when the blood is pushing too weakly against the walls of the blood vessels. As a result, blood does not circulate as well through the body. This can cause dizziness or a feeling of weakness.

### What you can do at home

What to do

- Raise your legs higher than your heart. For example, place a cushion under the legs, or sit down and lower the head below the knees.
- Get up slowly after sitting or lying down.
- Drink enough fluids every day, unless a healthcare professional advises otherwise.
- In the morning or with meals, have a drink containing caffeine, such as coffee or tea, unless a healthcare professional advises otherwise.

À éviter

- Avoid drinking alcohol.
- Avoid very hot showers and baths.
- Avoid hot tubs and saunas.
- Avoid staying still for long periods of time.
- Avoid crossing your legs while sitting.

### When to seek medical attention

Feeling faint with at least 1 of the following symptoms:

- paleness
- sweating
- weakness
- nausea
- blurred vision
- noise in the ears, such as whistling or buzzing (ringing)

Need help?

Call Info-Santé at 811, Option 1, to speak with a nurse at any time.

Go to the pharmacy for advice on medications or treatment.

Make an appointment at your medical clinic or CLSC.

If you do not have a family doctor, go to [gap.soinsvirtuels](https://gap.soinsvirtuels.gouv.qc.ca/en) or call 811, Option 3.

### When to go to the hospital right away

Confusion, agitation, or unusual behaviour

Hallucinations (seeing or hearing things that are not real)

Unusual tiredness (drowsiness) or difficulty staying awake

New chest or stomach pain

Dizziness or severe weakness that does not go away, even after following this advice

Loss of consciousness (fainting)

Severe difficulty breathing, especially with:

- shortness of breath that keeps getting worse, even at rest
- a high-pitched or whistling sound when breathing (stridor)
- the chest or abdomen pulling inward with each breath (retractions)

## Nosebleed (https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/nosebleed/)

Official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/nosebleed/

Epistaxis

### What it is

A nosebleed occurs when blood flows from one or both nostrils. It happens when small blood vessels inside the nose break. Most of the time, it is not serious and the bleeding stops on its own.

### What you can do at home

What to do

- Move to a quiet place without noise or commotion.
- Sit down and lean the head slightly forward.

**To stop the bleeding**

- Breathe through your mouth.
- Pinch the soft part of the nose, just below the bony bridge, for 10 to 15 minutes without releasing the pressure.
- Apply cold.
- Use a cold compress, an ice pack, a bag of frozen vegetables or a cold gel pack.
- Always place a cloth between the skin and the cold source to protect the skin.
- If the bleeding continues after 10 to 15 minutes, pinch the nose again for another 10 to 15 minutes.

**Once the bleeding has stopped**

- Do not rub or pick inside your nose.
- Avoid spicy foods and very hot drinks.
- Avoid strenuous physical activity for 24 hours.
- Try to avoid blowing your nose for 24 hours.

For people taking blood thinners:  monitor the bleeding and follow the recommendations provided by a healthcare professional.

### When to seek medical attention

Saignements de nez qui reviennent souvent, même après avoir suivi les conseils

Nosebleeds that keep coming back, even after following the advice provided

Need help?

Call Info-Santé at 811, Option 1, to speak with a nurse at any time.

Go to the pharmacy for advice on medications or treatment.

Make an appointment at your medical clinic or CLSC.

If you do not have a family doctor, go to [gap.soinsvirtuels](https://gap.soinsvirtuels.gouv.qc.ca/en) or call 811, Option 3.

### When to go to the hospital right away

Heavy bleeding that does not stop after 30 minutes, even after following the advice provided

Signs of severe bleeding:

- Severe weakness or feeling very unwell
- Skin that is paler than usual
- Cold or clammy skin
- A rapid heartbeat
- Rapid or difficult breathing, or shortness of breath

A nosebleed caused by a forceful impact or an injury to the head

### What it is

Numbness is when a part of the body, such as an arm or a leg, loses some feeling. It may tingle or become harder to move.

### What you can do at home

What to do

What to avoid

### When to seek medical attention

Numbness that starts gradually and gets worse

## Pinworms (enterobiasis) (https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/pinworms-enterobiasis/)

Official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/pinworms-enterobiasis/

Enterobius vermicularis

### C’est quoi

Pinworm infection, also known as enterobiasis, is a common infection that is not serious. It is caused by small white worms in the intestine and often causes no symptoms. With a new infection, small worms may be visible in the stool (poop), and itching may occur around the anus, especially at night.

### What you can do at home

What to do

- Ask a pharmacist about treatment for the infected person and anyone who lives with them or has had close contact with them.
- Everyone should start treatment at the same time.
- Repeat the treatment after 2 weeks.
- Follow the pharmacist’s treatment instructions.
- If the area is itchy, ask a pharmacist for advice before applying an ointment, cream, or lotion.

**On the first day of treatment**

- Wash bedding, towels, and washcloths in hot water.
- Vacuum the bedrooms.

**To prevent spreading the infection**

- Wash hands often, especially after using the toilet and before eating.
- Keep nails short and clean.
- Avoid putting fingers in the mouth, biting nails, or scratching around the anus.
- Clean the toilet seat thoroughly.
- Take a shower every day, preferably in the morning.
- Change pyjamas every day.
- Change underwear every day and always put on clean underwear after showering.
- Do not shake clothing or bedding, as this may spread the eggs.
- Regularly clean and disinfect frequently touched surfaces and objects, such as toys.
- For babies and young children: Use mittens or close-fitting underwear to prevent scratching.

À éviter

- Baths as the water may contain pinworm eggs.

### When to seek medical attention

Ask a pharmacist whether treatment is needed

A child under 2 years of age has symptoms or lives with someone who has a pinworm infection

A pregnant or breastfeeding person has symptoms or lives with someone who has a pinworm infection.

Symptoms are still present after the second treatment

Severe itching around the anus or genital area lasts more than a week despite following the recommended advice

The skin around the scratched area becomes red or swollen

Need help?

Call Info-Santé at 811, Option 1, to speak with a nurse at any time.

Go to the pharmacy for advice on medications or treatment.

Make an appointment at your medical clinic or CLSC.

If you do not have a family doctor, go to [gap.soinsvirtuels](https://gap.soinsvirtuels.gouv.qc.ca/en) or call 811, Option 3.

## Pollen allergy (https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/pollen-allergy/)

Official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/pollen-allergy/

### What it is

It happens when the body reacts to pollen in the air. It can cause a runny nose, sneezing, breathing problems, eye irritation, and skin reactions.

### What you can do at home

What to do

- Keep doors and windows closed.
- Go outside after it rains, if possible.
- Wash your hair and clothes after spending time outdoors.
- Ask a pharmacist whether over-the-counter medications may help.

What to avoid

- Going outside when it is very hot and windy.
- Contact with plants that cause allergies (e.g., ragweed).
- Using air conditioning systems that bring in outdoor air.
- Drying laundry outdoors.
- Mowing the lawn or raking leaves.

### When to seek medical attention

Ear pain that persists or gets worse

A persistent fever

Yellow nasal discharge that does not improve after 3 days

Allergy symptoms that do not improve after 7 days:

- Headache or pain around the eyes
- Tooth pain

Need help?

Call Info-Santé at 811, Option 1, to speak with a nurse at any time.

### When to go to the hospital right away

Difficulty breathing, especially if:

- Breathing is becoming increasingly difficult, even at rest.
- There is a high-pitched sound when breathing (stridor).
- The stomach or chest pulls in with each breath (retractions).

Fever in people who are more vulnerable to complications:

- People with a weakened immune system (immunocompromised).
- Pregnant people.
- Infants under 3 months old.

## Roseola (sixth disease) (https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/roseola-sixth-disease/)

Official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/roseola-sixth-disease/

Roseola infantum

### What it is

Roseola is a contagious viral illness that mainly affects children under 4 years of age. It is usually harmless and does not always have symptoms. The main symptoms are fever, runny nose, sore throat, and irritability. Small, non-itchy pink spots may also appear on the body.

### Quoi faire à la maison

À faire

- Drink small amounts of cool water often, unless a healthcare professional advises otherwise.
- Take acetaminophen (Tylenol) or ibuprofen (Advil). When in doubt, ask a pharmacist for advice.

Important: Ibuprofen is contraindicated for children under 6 months of age.

**To prevent spreading the infection**

- Wash hands often.
- Do not share items that have been in contact with the sick person’s mouth.
- Stay home during the most contagious period.
- Avoid close contact with other people, especially babies, children, older adults, and people who are immunocompromised.
- Regularly clean and disinfect frequently touched surfaces and objects.
