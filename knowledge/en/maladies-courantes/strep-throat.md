# Strep Throat

Official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/strep-throat/

Group A strep infection

## What it is

Official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/strep-throat/

A strep infection is contagious and usually causes a sore throat. It may also cause severe fatigue, headache, nausea, and fever. Scarlet fever may cause a red rash with small bumps and a red tongue that looks like a strawberry.

## What you can do at home

Official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/strep-throat/

What to do

- Get as much rest as possible.
- Replace the toothbrush 24 hours after starting antibiotics.
- To relieve pain, take acetaminophen (Tylenol) or ibuprofen (Advil). When in doubt, ask a pharmacist for advice.

Important: Ibuprofen is contraindicated for children under 6 months of age.

**To relieve a sore throat**

- For adults and children who can gargle: gargle with warm salt water for about 1 minute, several times a day.
    - Mix 240 mL (1 cup or 8 oz.) of safe drinking water or boiled water that has been left to cool with 2.5 mL (½ tsp.) of salt.
- Drink warm or cold fluids, whichever feels more soothing.
- Eat soft foods that are easy to swallow, such as soup, puréed foods, eggs, cottage cheese or yogurt.
- Try something cold, such as popsicles (ice pops) or frozen yogurt.
- For children aged 4 years and older: let an ice cube or a lozenge (cough drop) dissolve slowly in the mouth.
- For babies and young children: offer warm or cold water or milk, according to preference.

**To prevent spreading the infection**

- Stay home for the first 24 hours after starting antibiotics.
- Wash hands often.
- Do not share objects that have been in contact with the sick person's mouth.
- Regularly clean and disinfect frequently touched surfaces and objects.
- Avoid close contact with others, especially babies, children, older adults and people who are immunocompromised.

Avoid

- Smoking or vaping
- Places where there is smoke or dust

## When to seek medical attention

Official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/strep-throat/

Symptoms that last more than 3 days, even without known contact with someone who has a strep throat infection:

- severe sore throat
- pain when swallowing
- fever
- tender lymph nodes in the neck
- white patches on the tonsils or at the back of the throat

Nausea, vomiting or stomach pain, especially in children

After 3 days of antibiotics:

- persistent fever
- no improvement in overall condition

Fever, sore throat and difficulty swallowing after contact with someone who has a strep throat infection: [get tested](https://portal3.clicsante.ca/services/streptococcus-a-screening)

Need help?

Make an appointment through Clic Santé for [a screening](https://portal3.clicsante.ca/services/streptococcus-a-screening).

Call Info-Santé at 811, Option 1, to speak with a nurse at any time.

Go to the pharmacy for advice on medications or treatment.

Make an appointment at your medical clinic or CLSC.

If you do not have a family doctor, go [to https://gap.soinsvirtuels.gouv.qc.ca/en ↗](https://gap.soinsvirtuels.gouv.qc.ca/en) or call 811, Option 3.

## When to go to the hospital right away

Official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/strep-throat/

Inability to swallow saliva or water

Rapid changes in overall condition, including:

- unusual fatigue or difficulty staying awake
- little or no response to what is happening around them
- confusion, agitation or unusual behaviour
- hallucinations (seeing or hearing things that are not there)
- decreased appetite or thirst
- difficulty carrying out normal daily activities

Severe difficulty breathing, especially if:

- breathing becomes increasingly difficult, even at rest
- there is a high-pitched or wheezing sound when breathing (stridor)
- the chest or abdomen pulls inward with each breath (retractions)
- the skin, lips or fingers are blue or bluish (cyanosis)
- in babies and children under 2 years of age, the nostrils flare with each breath

Signs of dehydration that persist despite drinking plenty of fluids, including:

- dry mouth
- extreme thirst
- dark yellow urine
- no urine for more than 12 hours
- crying without tears
- weakness
- in young children, refusing to drink
