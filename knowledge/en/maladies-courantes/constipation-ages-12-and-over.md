# Constipation – Ages 12 and over

Official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/constipation-ages-12-and-over/

## What it is

Official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/constipation-ages-12-and-over/

Constipation is when it is hard to have a bowel movement (poop) or when bowel movements happen less often than usual. Stools (poop) may be hard. There may be stomach pain or a feeling of still needing to go, even after a bowel movement. Constipation is not a cause for concern, but it is important to find a way to get relief.

Related sheet available: [Constipation – Ages 0-11](https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/constipation-ages-0-11/)

## What you can do at home

Official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/constipation-ages-12-and-over/

What to do

- Massage the abdomen.
- Ask a pharmacist for advice before using any constipation treatment.
- Check with a pharmacist whether constipation could be a side effect of a medication.

**Making bowel movements easier**

- On the toilet, elevate your feet by placing them on a small stool.
- Choose a quiet, private bathroom.
- Breathe out (let the air out of the lungs) while pushing.
- Try to go to the toilet at the same times every day (regular schedule).

**Staying hydrated and eating well**

- Drink water throughout the day, unless a healthcare professional advises otherwise.
    - Ages 12 to 64: 2 to 3 litres of water a day
    - Ages 65 and over: 1.6 to 2 litres of water a day
- Eat high-fiber foods, such as fruit, vegetables, whole grains, prunes, and legumes.
- Eat small meals.
- Chew food thoroughly.
- Drink 120 mL of prune or fig juice 30 minutes before meals.
- Drink hot water, with or without lemon juice, 30 minutes before meals.

**Staying active**

- While standing, tighten and then relax the stomach muscles several times a day.
- Get at least 20 minutes of physical activity a day, such as walking, swimming, or cycling.
- Plan a 20-to-30-minute walk after a meal every day.
- For people aged 65 and over: ask for help getting around when needed, to stay active.
- For people with reduced mobility:
- Move in whatever ways are possible. Even small movements can help the bowels work properly
- If needed, get help with certain exercises (passive exercises).

What to avoid

- Do not use medications to help with bowel movements (laxatives) without medical advice.
- Ask a pharmacist for advice before using any constipation treatment.

## When to seek medical attention

Official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/constipation-ages-12-and-over/

No bowel movement (constipation) for 5 to 7 days, even after following this advice

Constipation that does not improve or gets worse

Constipation that turns into diarrhea (watery stools)

Frequent pain in the anus when having a bowel movement

Fever in a person who has had diverticulitis

Unintentional, unexplained weight loss

Blood in the stool (even a small amount)

Stools that are often small, watery, or hard

Ages 65 and over:

- change in bowel habits
- fewer bowel movements
- change in stool size or shape

Ages 40 and over: unexplained change in bowel habits, for example:

- less frequent bowel movements (constipation)
- change in the shape or appearance of stools

Need help?

Call Info-Santé at 811, Option 1, to speak with a nurse at any time.

Go to the pharmacy for advice on medications or treatment.

Make an appointment at your medical clinic or CLSC.

If you do not have a family doctor, go to [gap.soinsvirtuels](https://gap.soinsvirtuels.gouv.qc.ca/en) or call 811, Option 3.

## When to go to the hospital right away

Official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/constipation-ages-12-and-over/

A lot of red or black blood in the stool

Frequent vomiting or green or yellow-green vomit

Vomit that looks like and has a similar colour to stool (poop)

Severe stomach pain and a very hard abdomen for more than 2 hours

Rapid changes in overall condition:

- unusual tiredness (drowsiness) or difficulty staying awake
- less responsive to what is happening around them
- confusion, agitation, or unusual behaviour
- hallucinations (seeing or hearing things that are not real)
- reduced appetite or thirst
- difficulty carrying out usual daily activities
