# Food Allergies or Intolerances

Official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/food-allergies-or-intolerances/

Reaction to food

## What is it

Official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/food-allergies-or-intolerances/

A food allergy occurs when the body overreacts to a food, even after a very small amount is eaten, touched or inhaled. It can cause skin redness, vomiting and difficulty breathing. It can be dangerous. A food intolerance occurs when the body has trouble digesting a certain food. It mainly causes stomach aches, nausea or diarrhea (watery stools). It is not dangerous.

Related sheets available: [Allergy](https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/allergy/), [Anaphylactic Shock](https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/anaphylactic-shock/)

## What you can do at home

Official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/food-allergies-or-intolerances/

What to do

- Read the label every time you buy a product. If in doubt, avoid buying/eating the food completely.
- Be aware of hidden sources of allergens, such as:
    - Makeup
    - Cosmetics
    - Sunscreen
    - Medications
    - Pet food
- Wash your hands, utensils and surfaces with soap and water after touching the food that causes the reaction.
- Be careful at buffets and in other situations where foods may come into contact with one another.
- If you have a food intolerance, follow the recommendations provided by healthcare professional.

**When introducing foods to babies**

- Introduce one new food at a time, in a small amount.
- Wait 2 days before trying another new food.
- Introduce allergenic foods (e.g., eggs and fish) at the recommended time without delaying, unless a healthcare professional says otherwise.
- If the baby has eczema or a known allergy, consult a healthcare professional before introducing new foods.
- If symptoms appear in the baby after breastfeeding, note when they begin and what symptoms occur, then discuss them with a healthcare professional.

**If you have a known allergy**

- See the [Allergy health advice](https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/allergy/) page to learn:
    - how to recognize a severe allergic reaction;
    - when to use epinephrine (e.g., EpiPen, Allerject or Emerade);
    - when to to go to the hospital right away.

See the [Anaphylactic Shock](https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/anaphylactic-shock/) advice page in case of a very severe allergic reaction.

Avoid

- Avoid the food that causes the allergy or intolerance.
- Avoid foods sold in bulk.
- Do not share food or utensils.
- Do not cook different foods in the same oil.

## When to seek medical attention

Official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/food-allergies-or-intolerances/

The same symptoms occur every time after eating or touching the same food:

- skin redness
- nausea or vomiting
- stomach aches
- diarrhea (watery stools)

Need help?

Call Info-Santé at 811, Option 1, to speak with a nurse at any time.

Go to the pharmacy for advice on medications or treatment.

Make an appointment at your medical clinic or CLSC.

If you do not have a family doctor, go to [gap.soinsvirtuels](https://gap.soinsvirtuels.gouv.qc.ca/en) or call 811, Option 3.

## When to go to the hospital right away

Official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/food-allergies-or-intolerances/

After receiving epinephrine (e.g., EpiPen, Allerject or Emerade), even if symptoms improve or go away

After contact with a food that has previously caused a severe allergic reaction

Symptoms of a severe allergic reaction (redness, itchy skin, or swelling of the lips, tongue or face), along with at least one of the following:

- A high-pitched sound when breathing (stridor) or difficulty breathing
- Bluish or purplish lips or fingers
- Feeling weak or dizzy, or loss of consciousness
- A very rapid heartbeat
- Stomach ache, vomiting or diarrhea (watery stools)
- For young children (ages 0 to 2): not responding as usual, being limp or difficult to wake
