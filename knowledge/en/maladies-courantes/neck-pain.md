# Neck Pain

Official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/neck-pain/

Acute musculoskeletal pain

## What it is

Official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/neck-pain/

Neck pain is stiffness or discomfort in the neck. It can occur after staying in an awkward position for a long time, after a sudden awkward movement, or for no clear reason. It can be very painful and spread to the shoulders or arms.

Related sheet available: [Back Pain](https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/back-pain/)

## What you can do at home

Official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/neck-pain/

What to do

- Avoid straining the neck.
- Sleep on your back, keeping your head aligned with the body.
- Place a pillow under your head and under your knees.
- Maintain good posture during daily activities.
- When sitting, rest your back against the backrest and place your arms on the armrests.
- Avoid sitting for long periods of time without moving.
- When sitting for a long time is necessary, gently move the neck and take short walks from time to time.
- Continue physical activity based on how the pain progresses.

**Relieving pain**

- Apply a hot or cold compress, whichever feels better.
- Always place a cloth between the skin and the heat or cold source to protect the skin.
- Apply a cold compress for 15 to 20 minutes every 2 to 3 hours.
- Use a cold compress, such as, an ice pack, a bag of frozen vegetables, or a cold gel pack.

Or

- Apply a hot compress for 20 to 30 minutes, 4 times a day. Use a damp towel warmed in the microwave, a warm compress, or a hot water bottle.
- To reduce pain, take acetaminophen (Tylenol) or ibuprofen (Advil). When in doubt, ask a pharmacist for advice.

Important: Ibuprofen is contraindicated for children under 6 months of age.

Avoid

- Do not wear a cervical collar unless advised by a healthcare professional.
- Avoid sleeping on your stomach.

## When to seek medical attention

Official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/neck-pain/

Pain that gets worse or does not improve, even after following this advice

Neck pain caused by an injury (minor trauma), with pain lasting 2 to 4 weeks

Pain or weakness in the legs lasting more than 3 days

Neck pain with no known cause (no fall, accident, or trauma) in a person who:

- is under 18 or over 50 years old
- has had cancer
- has lost weight without trying

Need help?

Call Info-Santé at 811, Option 1, to speak with a nurse at any time.

Go to the pharmacy for advice on medications or treatment.

Make an appointment at your medical clinic or CLSC.

If you do not have a family doctor, go to [gap.soinsvirtuels](https://gap.soinsvirtuels.gouv.qc.ca/en) or call 811, Option 3.

## When to go to the hospital right away

Official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/neck-pain/

Severe neck pain that does not improve or gets worse, even after following this advice

Neck pain after an accident or major trauma (e.g., a car accident)

Fever

A red, swollen neck that feels warm, with no recent injury or accident

Neck pain with one or more of the following symptoms:

- stomach or chest pain
- being unable to move around
- leg pain, numbness, weakness, or loss of feeling in a body part
