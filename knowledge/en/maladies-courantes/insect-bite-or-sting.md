# Insect Bite or Sting

Official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/insect-bite-or-sting/

## What it is

Official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/insect-bite-or-sting/

An insect bite or sting occurs when an insect (such as a tick, mosquito, wasp, or bee) pierces the skin and injects venom or saliva. It can cause pain, redness, swelling, or itching. Most bites and stings are harmless, but some can transmit disease.

## What you can do at home

Official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/insect-bite-or-sting/

What to do

- If the bite is caused by a black widow spider, call the Poison Control Centre: 1- 800- 463- 5060.

**Removing a stinger**

- Remove the stinger promptly by gently scraping the skin.
- Wash the area with soap and water.

**Tick bites**

- Using tweezers, grasp the tick close to the skin and pull gently.
- Avoid crushing the tick during removal.
- Place the tick in a small, sealed container. Record the date, time, and location of the bite.
- Wash the area with soap and water.

Call Info-Santé 811, option 1, to report the tick bite.

**Relieving pain**

- Apply ice for 10 minutes every 30 minutes for 4 hours. Wrap the ice in a cloth to prevent skin damage.
- If swelling is present, remove jewelry, rings, or bracelets near the bite or sting.
- Elevate the affected arm or leg above heart level.
- Take acetaminophen (Tylenol) or ibuprofen (Advil). If in doubt, consult a pharmacist.

Important: Ibuprofen is contraindicated for children under 6 months of age.

**Relieving itching**

- Avoid scratching, even if the area is itchy.
- Apply a cold washcloth or a paste made of equal parts water and baking soda. Leave it on for a few seconds while applying gentle pressure.
- Ask a pharmacist whether over-the-counter medications or creams may help.

## When to seek medical attention

Official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/insect-bite-or-sting/

If you have any of these signs:

- A tick cannot be removed completely.
- Pain, itching, or swelling persists despite following these recommendations.
- The skin around the bite or sting becomes red, swollen, warm, or develops pus (yellow-green fluid).

Need help?

Call Info-Santé at 811, Option 1, to speak with a nurse at any time.

Go to the pharmacy for advice on medications or treatment.

Make an appointment at your medical clinic or CLSC.

If you do not have a family doctor, go to [gap.soinsvirtuels](https://gap.soinsvirtuels.gouv.qc.ca/en) or call 811, Option 3.

## When to go to the hospital right away

A fever after a recent trip

(within the past 3 months) to a country where malaria is a risk  [Government of Canada](https://www.canada.ca/en/public-health/services/diseases/malaria/risks-malaria.html).

The skin around the bite or sting turns black.

A severe allergic reaction to an insect bite or sting has occurred in the past.

Symptoms of a severe allergic reaction are present (rash, itchy skin, or swelling of the lips, tongue, or face), along with at least one of the following:

- high-pitched breathing sounds (stridor) or difficulty breathing;
- bluish lips or fingers;
- weakness, dizziness, or loss of consciousness (fainting);
- rapid heartbeat;
- abdominal pain, vomiting, or diarrhea (loose, watery stools).

A wasp, hornet, or bumblebee sting occurs inside the mouth.

A sting occurs near the eye and is accompanied by pain or difficulty seeing.

Multiple insect bites or stings occur at the same time.

Following a tick bite, any of the following develop:

- headache;
- stiff neck;
- fever;
- sensitivity to light;
- vomiting;
- weakness;
- severe pain;
- chest pain;
- difficulty breathing;
- loss of consciousness (fainting);
- rapid heartbeat;
- sudden numbness.
