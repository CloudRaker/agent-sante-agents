## YOUR ONCOLOGY PASSPORT: A VALUABLE TOOL FOR YOU AND YOUR LOVED ONES

Official source: https://publications.msss.gouv.qc.ca/msss/fichiers/2025/25-902-06A.pdf

You have your oncology passport in hand. It will help you and your loved ones understand your treatment program, follow its progress and actively participate in it. Throughout the stages of your care journey, it will make it easier for you to manage your appointment schedule with your doctors, nurses, pharmacists and other health professionals, your treatment visits and the examinations that will be prescribed for you.

Your passport also contains information about the symptoms you might experience during your care journey. It will help you decide when and whom to see if you experience some of these symptoms. You can use it to record a wealth of information about your tests, examinations, symptoms and medications. Bring it with you every time you visit the hospital, doctor, CLSC and emergency room and show it to care providers so they can consult it and provide the necessary follow-up.

Finally, your oncology passport belongs to you, much like what you are going through at the moment. Use it as you see fit. Do not hesitate to share any concerns, questions or comments with your resource people.1 – PERSONAL INFORMATION AND TELEPHONE NUMBERS

## 6 – WEIGHT AND BLOOD TESTS

Official source: https://publications.msss.gouv.qc.ca/msss/fichiers/2025/25-902-06A.pdf

You may receive certain drugs that reduce bloodcell production. We will test your blood regularly to make sure your cell count is not low. Other tests may also be conducted.

|**Weight:**|Used to adjust the dosage of your chemotherapy.|
|---|---|
|**Hemoglobin:**|Provides information about your red blood cells’ ability to carry oxygen from the lungs to the rest of the body.<br>If the value is too low, you may experience symptoms such as acute fatigue or weakness, dizziness,<br>orpersistent shortness of breath.|
|**Neutrophils:**|Provides information about your white blood cells’ ability to fight infections.<br>If the count is too low,you maydevelopan infection, which often results in fever.|
|**Platelets:**|Help produce blood clots, which prevent prolonged bleeding in the event of injury.<br>If the count is too low,you maybleed or bruise more than usual.|
