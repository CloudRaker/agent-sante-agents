# Superficial wound

Official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/superficial-wound/

Abrasion, Scrape, Scratch, or Skin Tear

## What is it

Official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/superficial-wound/

These are usually minor injuries caused by friction that damages the skin. Examples include abrasions (scrapes), scratches, or tears affecting the surface layer of the skin.

## What you can do at home

Official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/superficial-wound/

What to do

- Wash hands with soap and water before touching the wound.
- To reduce pain, take acetaminophen (Tylenol) or ibuprofen (Advil). When in doubt, ask a pharmacist for advice.

Important: Ibuprofen is contraindicated for children under 6 months of age.

**If the wound is bleeding heavily**

- Cover the wound with a bandage or clean cloth.
- Apply pressure for 15 minutes.
- Once the bleeding has slowed, gently clean the wound.

**If there is debris or dirt in the wound**

- Use a compress soaked in saline solution (store-bought or homemade), or running tap water, to remove debris from the wound.
- To prepare a homemade saline solution:
    - Mix 240 mL (8 oz) of drinking water or boiled water.
    - Add 2.5 mL (½ teaspoon) of salt.
- Once the wound is clear of debris, gently clean the wound.

**Cleaning the wound**

- Clean the wound with tap water and mild, unscented soap, or with a saline solution purchased from a pharmacy or prepared at home, for a few minutes, then rinse.
- If a flap of skin remains attached, carefully reposition it over the wound.
- Cover the wound with a bandage.
- Change the bandage once a day, or sooner if it becomes wet or dirty.

## When to seek medical attention

Official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/superficial-wound/

- Seek medical care within 24 hours if sand, dust, metal, glass, or plastic cannot be removed from the wound.
- Seek medical care within 48 hours if a thorn or splinter cannot be removed from the wound.
- There is a sensation that something is still under the skin.
- The wound is located on a joint, such as the knee, or on a hand or foot.
- The wound is wide and/or deep.
- The wound is large:
    - For an adult, it is longer than 2.5 cm (1 inch).
    - For a child, it is longer than 2.5 cm (1 inch).
- The wound does not appear to be healing or is getting larger.
- Pain does not improve after 3 days.
- The skin around the wound becomes red, swollen, warm, or drains pus (yellow-green fluid).

It is important to ensure that the injured person's [tetanus](https://www.quebec.ca/en/health/health-issues/a-z/tetanus) vaccination is up to date.

Need help?

Call Info-Santé 811, option 1, to speak with a nurse at any time.

Consult a pharmacist for advice on medications or treatment.

Book an appointment on [Clic Santé](https://portal3.clicsante.ca/) if vaccination is needed.

Book an appointment at your medical clinic or CLSC.

If you do not have a family doctor, go to [gap.soinsvirtuels](https://gap.soinsvirtuels.gouv.qc.ca/en) or call 811, option 3.

## When to go to the hospital immediately

Official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/superficial-wound/

Fever, chills, or shaking

The wound or the surrounding area becomes red and warm rapidly (within a few hours)

The wound or the surrounding area continues to be very painful

A very rapid heartbeat

Heavy bleeding that does not stop despite 15 minutes of continuous pressure
