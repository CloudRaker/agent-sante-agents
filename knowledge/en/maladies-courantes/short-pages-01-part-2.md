# Short pages, theme maladies-courantes (1/2): the official source of each page is in its section title

This file groups short official pages. Each section is one page. Cite the URL of the section, not this file.

### When to seek medical attention

Small red spots appear on your body that last 3 days or more

Fever that returns after being gone for 24 hours, despite following the recommended advice

Your skin or the whites of your eyes turn yellow (jaundice)

White or pale grey stools

Need help?

Call Info-Santé at 811, Option 1, to speak with a nurse at any time.

Go to the pharmacy for advice on medications or treatment.

Make an appointment at your medical clinic or CLSC.

If you do not have a family doctor, go to [gapsoinsvirtuels](https://gap.soinsvirtuels.gouv.qc.ca/en) or call 811, Option 3.

### When to go to the hospital right away

Rapid changes in the person’s condition:

- unusual fatigue (drowsiness) or difficulty staying awake;
- reduced responsiveness to what is happening around them;
- confusion, agitation, or unusual behaviour;
- hallucinations (seeing or hearing things that are not real);
- reduced appetite or thirst;

Seizures (uncontrollable shaking of the body)

A very severe headache with a stiff neck, vomiting or sensitivity to light

## Rubella (https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/rubella/)

Official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/rubella/

### What it is

Rubella is a viral infection that may not cause any symptoms. When symptoms do occur, they may include a mild fever, red eyes, and small red spots on the skin. The illness is usually mild, but it can be dangerous during pregnancy.

### What you can do at home

What to do

- Rest.
- Choose calm activities.
- Drink plenty of water.
- Eat according to appetite. Have smaller meals more often, if needed.
- Take acetaminophen (Tylenol) or ibuprofen (Advil). When in doubt, ask a pharmacist for advice.

Important: Ibuprofen is contraindicated for children under 6 months of age.

**To prevent spreading the infection**

- Wash hands often.
- Regularly clean and disinfect frequently touched surfaces and objects.
- Avoid contact with other people, especially pregnant people.

What to avoid

- Touching the nose or mouth of a contagious person
- Sharing drinking glasses or utensils with a contagious person

### When to seek medical attention

For a person aged 6 months or older

Vaccination may be recommended within 3 days of contact with someone who has rubella

For a pregnant person

After contact with someone who has rubella

The measles, mumps, rubella and chickenpox (varicella) vaccine has not been received or is not up to date

Make an appointment through [Clic Santé](https://portal3.clicsante.ca/services/measles-mumps-chickenpox-and-rubella-vaccination) if vaccination is needed

Need help?

Call Info-Santé at 811, Option 1, to speak with a nurse at any time.

Go to the pharmacy for advice on medications or treatment.

Make an appointment at your medical clinic or CLSC.

If you do not have a family doctor, go to [gap.soinsvirtuels](https://gap.soinsvirtuels.gouv.qc.ca/en) or call 811, Option 3.

### When to go to the hospital right away

Rapid changes in the person’s condition:

- unusual fatigue (drowsiness) or difficulty staying awake
- reduced responsiveness to what is happening around them
- confusion, agitation, or unusual behaviour
- hallucinations (seeing or hearing things that are not real)
- reduced appetite or thirst
- difficulty carrying out normal daily activities

## Sprains (https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/sprains/)

Official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/sprains/

Acute musculoskeletal pain

### What it is

A sprain happens when a ligament, which connects the bones in a joint, is stretched or torn after a sudden movement or a fall. It often causes pain and may also cause swelling and difficulty moving the affected area. The swelling can sometimes be significant.

### What you can do at home

What to do

- If needed, use crutches or a cane to help you walk.
- Gradually return to your usual activities.
- To help relieve pain, take acetaminophen (Tylenol) or ibuprofen (Advil). When in doubt, ask a pharmacist for advice.

Important: Ibuprofen is contraindicated for children under 6 months of age.

**To reduce swelling**

- Apply an elastic bandage around the injury, but do not wrap it too tightly.
- Remove and reapply the bandage a few times during the day and remove it at night.
- Keep the injured area elevated above the level of your heart as much as possible.

**Sprain after a fall**

- Get into a comfortable position.
- If needed, ask for help.

**Apply a cold compress**

- Apply a cold compress for 15 to 20 minutes every 2 to 3 hours.
- Use a cold compress, ice pack, bag of frozen vegetables, or cooling gel pack.
- Always place a cloth between your skin and the cold source to protect the skin.

What to avoid

- Do not make any sudden movements.

### When to seek medical attention

Unable to put weight on the injured leg or arm.

Difficulty resuming usual activities.

Pain that continues 2 to 4 weeks after the sprain.

Need help?

Call Info-Santé at 811, Option 1, to speak with a nurse at any time.

Go to the pharmacy for advice on medications or treatment.

Make an appointment at your medical clinic or CLSC.

If you do not have a family doctor, go to [gap.soinsvirtuels](https://gap.soinsvirtuels.gouv.qc.ca/en) or call 811, Option 3.

### When to go to the hospital right away

A cracking or tearing sound was heard at the time of the accident.

The injured body part is deformed or has an abnormal angle.

The injured joint is twice its normal size, even after applying cold.

Numbness, tingling, or loss of sensation is present in the injured area.

## Stomach ache (https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/stomach-ache/)

Official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/stomach-ache/

Abdominal pain

### What it is

Abdominal pain is pain or discomfort felt in the area between the chest and the pelvis. It can take different forms, including cramping, stabbing pain, a pulling sensation, burning, or tightness. In children, most episodes of abdominal pain are not serious and go away on their own after a period of rest.

### What you can do at home

What to do

- Rest in a comfortable position.
- Stay in a calm environment with minimal noise and stress.

**Relieving pain**

- Apply something cold to the abdomen, such as a cold washcloth.
- Hold a pillow or cushion against the abdomen.

What to avoid

- Do not apply heat.
- Do not eat or drink if the pain is severe, constant, or rapidly increasing.
- Do not take medications, laxatives (products used to promote bowel movements), or use an enema without advice from a healthcare professional (physician or pharmacist).

### When to seek medical attention

- Abdominal pain that occurs repeatedly or lasts more than 24 hours (1 day)
- Fever
- Pink, red, or bloody urine
- Recurring upper abdominal pain, such as a burning sensation lasting, despite following these recommendations.

### When to go to the hospital right away

Abdominal pain has been constant for more than 2 hours.

The pain is severe and started suddenly.

The pain is increasing rapidly, even at rest.

Pain is felt under the ribs (in the upper abdomen) along with one or more of the following:

- excessive sweating;
- difficulty breathing;
- dizziness;
- extreme fatigue or unusual weakness;
- nausea or vomiting;
- rapid heartbeat or palpitations.

Stools are red or black and tar-like.

Vomit contains blood (red or resembling coffee grounds) or resembles stool.

Vomit is green or yellow-green, and the abdomen is hard and swollen.

Fever is present in people at higher risk:

- weakened immune system (immunocompromised);
- pregnancy;
- babies younger than 3 months of age.
