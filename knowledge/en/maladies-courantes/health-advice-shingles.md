# Shingles

Official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/shingles/

## What it is

Official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/shingles/

Shingles occurs when the chickenpox virus becomes active again in someone who has previously had chickenpox. Painful spots appear, often on only one side of the body. Symptoms may last 2 to 3 weeks.

Related sheet available: [Chickenpox](https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/chickenpox/)

## What you can do at home

Official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/shingles/

What to do

- To relieve pain, take acetaminophen (Tylenol). When in doubt, follow the advice provided by a pharmacist.

**Caring for the spots**

- Wash the spots daily with a mild soap and rinse thoroughly.
- Pat the skin dry. Do not rub.
- Do not remove firmly attached scabs. Remove only loose scabs that catch on clothing.
- Cover all spots with a bandage to reduce the risk of spreading the virus to someone else.

**Itchy skin**

- Wear loose, soft clothing.
- For children aged 0 to 2 years: Use mittens as needed to prevent scratching.
- Apply cold, wet compresses to the skin for 30 to 90 minutes every 2 to 4 hours, as needed.
- Moisturize the skin after a bath or shower, for example with a lanolin-based cream, petroleum jelly, or mineral oil, unless this makes the rash worse.
- Take a 10- to 30-minute lukewarm bath without soap, bubble bath, or scented products 3 to 4 times a day.
- If the skin is dry, take only one 10-minute bath a day.
- If the skin is not dry, add baking soda to the bathwater: 50 to 60 mL for children under 2 years of age, or 250 to 500 mL for adults and children aged 2 years and older.

Important: Never leave a child unattended in the bath.

**Spots in the mouth**

- Rinse the mouth with cold water, as needed.
- Drink cold fluids.
- Avoid acidic, spicy, salty, hard, or very hot foods.
- Brush teeth with a very soft toothbrush.
- Avoid mouthwash containing alcohol.
- For young children: Use a cup, spoon, or syringe instead of a bottle because sucking increases mouth pain.
- For older children: Use a straw.
- From age 4: Rinse the mouth with a baking soda solution up to 4 times a day. Mix ½ teaspoon of baking soda with 1 cup (250 mL) of water that has been boiled for 1 minute and has fully cooled. Use 5 mL each time for children aged 4 to 6 years, 5 to 10 mL for children aged 6 to 12 years, and 15 mL from age 12.
- Apply an anesthetic gel in the mouth before eating. Ask a pharmacist for advice.

Important: Anesthetic gel is not recommended for young children. When in doubt, ask a pharmacist for advice.

**To prevent spreading the infection**

- Wash hands often.
- Do not share items that have been in contact with the sick person’s mouth.
- Stay home during the most contagious period.
- Avoid close contact with other people, especially babies, children, older adults, and people who are immunocompromised.
- Regularly clean and disinfect frequently touched surfaces and objects.

Avoid

- Scratching the skin
- Sun exposure, because heat makes itching worse
- Ibuprofen (Advil) or aspirin
- Swimming until all the blisters have turned into scabs.

## When to seek medical attention

Official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/shingles/

If shingles is suspected, seek medical attention within 72 hours because antiviral treatment may be recommended

Pain that continues despite following the recommended advice

Pain that continues 4 months after the shingles has cleared

Areas of spots that are getting larger

Blisters (fluid-filled bumps) that are still present after 3 weeks

Need help?

Call Info-Santé at 811, Option 1, to speak with a nurse at any time.

Go to the pharmacy for advice on medications or treatment.

Make an appointment at your medical clinic or CLSC.

If you do not have a family doctor, go to [gap.soinsvirtuels](https://gap.soinsvirtuels.gouv.qc.ca/en) or call 811, Option 3.

Make an appointment through Clic Santé if [vaccination is recommended](https://portal3.clicsante.ca/services/shingles-vaccination) based on age, health, or individual circumstances.

## When to go to the hospital right away

Official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/shingles/

Rapid changes in the person’s condition, such as:

- unusual fatigue or difficulty staying awake
- reduced responsiveness
- confusion, agitation, unusual behaviour
- reduced appetite or thirst
- difficulty carrying out normal daily activities

Severe headache with fever, vomiting, or extreme sensitivity to light

Spots that become red, swollen, warm, or filled with pus (yellow-green fluid) with:

- symptoms that worsen rapidly
- the onset of fever, chills, or weakness

Spots around the eye, with or without eye redness, or on the nose

Sudden difficulty seeing clearly
