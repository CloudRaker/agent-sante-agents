# Back Pain

Official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/back-pain/

Acute musculoskeletal pain

## What it is

Official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/back-pain/

Back pain is a feeling of stiffness or discomfort in the back. It can occur after physical exertion, after staying in an awkward position for a long time, after a fall, or for no obvious reason. The pain can be very severe and spread down into the legs.

Related sheet available: [Back Pain](https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/back-pain/)

## What you can do at home

Official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/back-pain/

What to do

- Rest your back.
- Lie down only to sleep.
- Maintain good, ergonomic posture during daily activities.
- Continue physical activity based on how the pain progresses.
- Choose activities such as walking, cycling, or swimming (no jumping).

**Alternate between comfortable positions during daily activities**

- When sitting:
    - Rest your back firmly against the backrest.
    - Place your arms on the armrests.
    - Put your feet on a stool so that your knees are higher than your hips.
    - Stay in this position for 20 to 50 minutes at most.
- Lie on your back, with a cushion under your head and another under your knees.
- Lie on your side with your knees bent, with a cushion under your head and another between your knees.
- Lie on your back on the floor, in a position that is comfortable for the lower back:
- Rest your legs on a chair so that your hips and knees are bent at about 90 degrees.
- Place a pillow under your head and another under your legs.
- Stay in this position for 5 to 25 minutes.

**Relieving pain**

- Apply a cold compress for 15 to 20 minutes every 2 to 3 hours.
- Use a cold compress, such as, an ice pack, a bag of frozen vegetables, or a cold gel pack.
- Always place a cloth between the skin and the cold source to protect the skin.
- To reduce pain, take acetaminophen (Tylenol) or ibuprofen (Advil). When in doubt, ask a pharmacist for advice.

Important: Ibuprofen is contraindicated for children under 6 months of age.

À éviter

- Avoid straining to lift objects that are too heavy.

## When to seek medical attention

Official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/back-pain/

Pain or weakness in the legs lasting more than 3 days

Back pain caused by an injury (minor trauma), with pain lasting 2 to 4 weeks

Pain that gets worse or does not improve, even after following this advice

Back pain with no known cause (no fall, accident, or trauma) in a person who:

- is under 18 or over 50
- has had cancer
- has lost weight without trying

Need help?

Call Info-Santé at 811, Option 1, to speak with a nurse at any time.

Go to the pharmacy for advice on medications or treatment.

Make an appointment at your medical clinic or CLSC.

If you do not have a family doctor, go to [gap.soinsvirtuels](https://gap.soinsvirtuels.gouv.qc.ca/en) or call 811, Option 3.

## When to go to the hospital right away

Official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/back-pain/

Severe back pain that does not improve or gets worse, even after following this advice

Back pain after an accident or trauma (e.g., a car accident)

Fever

A red, swollen back that feels warm, with no recent injury or accident

Back pain with one or more of the following symptoms:

- stomach or chest pain
- difficulty urinating (peeing) or pain when urinating
- blood in the urine (pee)
- unable to move around
- leaking urine or stool (poop) without meaning to
- pain, numbness, weakness, or loss of feeling in the legs
