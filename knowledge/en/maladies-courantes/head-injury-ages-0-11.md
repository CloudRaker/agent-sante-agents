# Head Injury – Ages 0-11

Official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/head-injury-ages-0-11/

## What is it

Official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/head-injury-ages-0-11/

A head injury occurs when the head receives a blow or impact. For example, an object may hit the head, or the head may hit the ground during a fall.

Related sheet available: [Head Injury – Ages 12 and over](https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/head-injury-ages-12-and-over/)

## What you can do at home

Official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/head-injury-ages-0-11/

What to do

- Reassure the child and make them comfortable.
- If there is a minor wound, gently wash it with tap water and mild, unscented soap (see the [_Superficial Wound_](https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/superficial-wound/) page).
- If there is a bump or bruise, apply a cold washcloth for 15 minutes (see the [_Bruise or Bump_](https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/bruise-or-bump-no-open-wound/) page).
- Cover the wound with a bandage.
- Change the bandage once a day or whenever it becomes dirty or wet.
- Choose restful activities and have small meals.
- To reduce pain, take acetaminophen (Tylenol). When in doubt, ask a pharmacist for advice.

**If the wound is bleeding heavily**

- Place a bandage or clean cloth over the wound.
- Apply pressure to the bandage for 15 minutes.
- Once the bleeding has slowed, gently wash the wound with tap water and mild, unscented soap for a few minutes, then rinse.
- Cover the wound with a bandage.
- Change the bandage once a day or whenever it becomes dirty or wet.

**Monitor symptoms**

- Monitor the child for the first 2 hours after the injury. Watch for:
    - headache;
    - dizziness;
    - nausea;
    - unusual tiredness;
    - blurred vision;
    - loss of balance.
- If it is nap or bedtime, let the child sleep. After 2 hours, wake the child to make sure they are walking, talking, and responding as usual.
- Continue to monitor for symptoms for 3 days.
- Inform the daycare or school, if needed.

## When to seek medical attention

Official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/head-injury-ages-0-11/

- The wound is open or deep and may need stitches.
- The skin around the wound becomes red, swollen, or warm, or there is pus (yellow-green fluid).
- The headache lasts for more than 3 days.
- The injury was caused by a rusty object.

It is important to ensure that the injured person's [tetanus](https://www.quebec.ca/en/health/health-issues/a-z/tetanus) vaccination is up to date.

Need help?

Call Info-Santé at 811, Option 1, to speak with a nurse at any time.

Go to the pharmacy for advice on medications or treatment.

Book an appointment on [Clic Santé](https://portal3.clicsante.ca/) if vaccination is needed.

Make an appointment at your medical clinic or CLSC.

If you do not have a family doctor, go to [gap.soinsvirtuels](https://gap.soinsvirtuels.gouv.qc.ca/en) or call 811, Option 3.

## When to go to the hospital right away

Official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/head-injury-ages-0-11/

The child fell from a height of more than 1 meter or fell down more than 5 steps.

The child lost consciousness.

The child’s behaviour changes, including:

- having difficulty waking up;
- walking unusually;
- having muscle weakness;
- having difficulty speaking;
- being very irritable;
- crying continuously for more than 10 minutes.

The wound continues to bleed even after applying pressure.

There is concern that the child may have been physically abused.

Within 3 days of the head injury, the child develops any of the following

- seizures;
- confusion;
- difficulty breathing;
- difficulty walking;
- problems with balance or muscle weakness;
- difficulty speaking;
- severe headache or neck pain;
- a bulging soft spot on the top of the head (fontanelle);
- fluid coming from the nose or ears;
- unequal pupils (the black circles in the eyes);
- tingling in an arm or leg;
- bruising behind the ear;
- a black eye.

Vomiting starts or continues more than 4 hours after the head injury.

For babies under 3 months:

 a large bruise (collection of blood or hematoma) on the scalp or forehead.

For kids 3 months to 2 years:

a large bruise (collection of blood or hematoma) measuring more than 2.5 cm on the scalp or forehead.
