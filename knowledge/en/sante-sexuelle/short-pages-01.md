## Hives (https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/hives/)

Official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/hives/

Red, Raised Patches

### What it is

Hives are raised patches or small bumps that appear quickly on the skin. They can vary in size and may be itchy. Hives can appear, disappear, or move to different areas of the body within a short period of time.

### What you can do at home

What to do

- Wear loose-fitting, soft clothing.
- Apply cool compresses to the skin for 30 to 90 minutes, every 2 to 4 hours as needed.
- Use a mild soap and apply an unscented moisturizer, unless it makes the hives worse.
- Ask a pharmacist whether an over-the-counter medication may help.

**Take lukewarm baths**

- Take lukewarm baths for 10 to 30 minutes, 3 to 4 times a day.
- For children 2 years of age and younger, add 50 to 60 mL (about ¼ cup) of baking soda to the bath water.
- For children over 2 years of age and adults, add 250 to 500 mL (1 to 2 cups) of baking soda to the bath water.

What to avoid

- If the cause of the hives is known, remove or avoid it. Identifying the cause may also help prevent hives from returning.
- Avoid scratching if the skin is itchy.

### When to seek medical attention

- Fever is present.
- Pain and swelling occur in the joints (for example, the knee, elbow, or wrist).

Children aged 2 to 11 years

- The itching is severe.
- The itching does not improve after 24 hours, even after taking an allergy medication (antihistamine), such as Aerius, Allegra, Benadryl, Claritin, or Reactine.

Individuals 12 years and older

Skin patches that:

- Appear and disappear without an obvious cause.
- May be caused by a recently started medication.
- Last longer than 7 days.
- Cover a large area of the body.

Need help?

Call Info-Santé at 811, Option 1, to speak with a nurse at any time.

Go to the pharmacy for advice on medications or treatment.

Make an appointment at your medical clinic or CLSC.

If you do not have a family doctor, go to [gap.soinsvirtuels](https://gap.soinsvirtuels.gouv.qc.ca/en) or call 811, Option 3.

### When to go to the hospital right away

Fever in high-risk individuals

- People with weakened immune systems (immunocompromised)
- Pregnant individuals
- Infants under 3 months of age

Symptoms of a severe allergic reaction (redness, itchy skin, swelling of the lips, tongue, or face), along with at least one of the following:

- High-pitched breathing sounds (stridor) or difficulty breathing
- Bluish or purplish lips or fingers
- Feeling weak or dizzy, or loss of consciousness
- Very fast heartbeat
- Stomach pain, vomiting, or diarrhea

## Access to abortion services (https://www.quebec.ca/en/health/health-system-and-services/service-organization/abortion-services/access-abortion-services)

Official source: https://www.quebec.ca/en/health/health-system-and-services/service-organization/abortion-services/access-abortion-services

### Time frame for obtaining a voluntary termination of pregnancy

A [medical abortion](https://www.quebec.ca/en/health/health-system-and-services/service-organization/abortion-services/description#c189655) is possible only during the first trimester of pregnancy.

A [surgical abortion](https://www.quebec.ca/en/health/health-system-and-services/service-organization/abortion-services/description#lv43249) is legal at any stage of pregnancy. However, services may vary from one facility to the next. It may be necessary to go to another region.

A referral from a doctor is not necessary for access to abortion services.

### Costs

Abortion services are **free** everywhere they are offered for:

- women who have a Quebec health insurance card;
- women who are covered by the [Interim Federal Health Program](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/refugees/help-within-canada/health-care/interim-federal-health-program/coverage-summary.html) (IFHP).

#### Other situations

Women who are not in one of the two categories mentioned above can have access to abortion services at their expense. The rates charged vary according to the stage of pregnancy, medical conditions, etc. The persons concerned will have to contact the [abortion clinics listed](https://sante.gouv.qc.ca/en/repertoire-ressources/avortement/) to find out their rates.

### Age of consent

Adolescents aged **14 and older** do not need permission from their parents to get an abortion. The decision on whether to pursue their pregnancy is theirs alone.

Adolescents aged **13 and under** must obtain permission from their parents or legal guardian to get an abortion. The parents must take into account the adolescent’s option and the decision must be made in the adolescent’s interest. If an adolescent and her parents disagree on the decision to make, a CLSC worker will be able to help them. If they are unable to reach an agreement, the court might be called upon to intervene and render a decision.

## Deciding to have an abortion (https://www.quebec.ca/en/health/health-system-and-services/service-organization/abortion-services/deciding-have-abortion)

Official source: https://www.quebec.ca/en/health/health-system-and-services/service-organization/abortion-services/deciding-have-abortion

Deciding whether to have an abortion is a personal choice. It is entirely up to the woman. She is free to make her own choice and does not need the permission of her partner or any other person to get an abortion. There are many reasons why a woman might choose to have an abortion. Each woman has her own reasons.

### Support of health professionals

Choosing to terminate a pregnancy might cause you to feel emotions and have concerns. Don’t hesitate to speak with the health professionals so they can answer your questions. The medical staff offering abortion services respect each woman’s choice and do not judge. They provide the abortion services and follow-up with neutrality, respect and complete confidentiality.

Community organizations that help with making pro-choice decisions are available to help you. They can share tools for reflection with you and help you make the decision that is right for you.

[SOS Grossesse Estrie](https://www.sosgrossesseestrie.qc.ca/) (in French only): [819-822-1181](tel:8198221181)

[Grossesse Secours Montréal](https://www.grossesse-secours.org/) (in French only): [514-271-0554](tel:5142710554)

[SOS Grossesse Québec](https://www.sosgrossesse.ca/) (in French only): [1-877-662-9666](tel:18776629666)

If you need **support after terminating a pregnancy**, contact [SOS Grossesse](https://www.sosgrossesse.ca/) at [1-877-662-9666](tel:18776629666).

## Obtaining abortion services (https://www.quebec.ca/en/health/health-system-and-services/service-organization/abortion-services/obtaining-abortion-services)

Official source: https://www.quebec.ca/en/health/health-system-and-services/service-organization/abortion-services/obtaining-abortion-services

### Procedure for obtaining abortion services

Before making an appointment, you will need to take a urine pregnancy test to ensure that you are, in fact, pregnant. You can buy a pregnancy test over the counter at a pharmacy or get one for free in some CLSCs. To find a CLSC or pharmacy near you, see the [Finding a CLSC](https://sante.gouv.qc.ca/en/repertoire-ressources/clsc/) page.

Once the pregnancy is confirmed, if you wish, you can talk to the available resources who will help you with making your decision.

If you decide to terminate your pregnancy, you will have to **make an appointment for an abortion**. To find a resource in your sector, see the [Finding a Resource Offering Abortion Services](https://sante.gouv.qc.ca/en/repertoire-ressources/avortement/) page.

When making the appointment, you will be asked the following information:

- your name;
- your telephone number;
- your health insurance number (RAMQ), if you have one;
- the first day of your last menstrual period, if you know it.

If you call outside opening hours, you will be able to leave a message by indicating the best time to reach you. The return call will be fully confidential.

### Help for adolescents

Adolescents can also consult a nurse at their school or in a CLSC for help. To find a CLSC, see the [Finding a CLSC](https://sante.gouv.qc.ca/en/repertoire-ressources/clsc/) page.

In any case, it is important to talk to a trusted person, such as a parent, teacher or the school social worker.
