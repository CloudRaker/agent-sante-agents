## Why get vaccinated against COVID-19

Official source: https://www.quebec.ca/en/health/advice-and-prevention/vaccination/covid-19-vaccine

COVID-19 vaccination reduces:

- the risk of complications of COVID-19;
- hospital admissions and deaths related to COVID-19.

It could also prevent symptoms that persist for many months, also known as [long COVID](https://www.quebec.ca/en/health/health-issues/a-z/2019-coronavirus/long-covid).

Go to the [COVID-19](https://www.quebec.ca/en/health/health-issues/a-z/2019-coronavirus) page to find out more.

## Who can get vaccinated free of charge in fall-winter 2026‑2027

Official source: https://www.quebec.ca/en/health/advice-and-prevention/vaccination/covid-19-vaccine

In fall-winter 2026‑2027, COVID-19 vaccination is offered free of charge to the following people:

- people who live in residential and long-term care centres (CHSLDs, Elders’ Houses) as well as in intermediate resources in support of seniors’ autonomy (RI-SAPA);
- people who live in private seniors’ residences (RPAs) or other communal settings with a high proportion of elderly and vulnerable people;
- people who are 65 years of age or older;
- people who are 6 months of age or older who are immunocompromised, on dialysis, or living with certain chronic diseases;
- people who are pregnant, regardless of the stage of pregnancy;
- adults living in remote and isolated areas;
- health care staff.

If in doubt, a health professional can help you determine if you are concerned.

## Who can pay to get vaccinated

Official source: https://www.quebec.ca/en/health/advice-and-prevention/vaccination/covid-19-vaccine

The COVID-19 vaccine is available for people 12 years of age and older which do not meet the criteria for being free and who want to reduce their risk of complications, but it is not offered free of charge. To find out how much the vaccine costs, contact your pharmacy or medical clinic.

#### Chronic diseases increasing the risk of COVID-19 complications

The vaccine is recommended to anyone living with any of the chronic diseases listed below because their disease makes them more vulnerable to complications from the COVID-19:

- Chronic cardiac or pulmonary disorders severe enough to require regular medical attention or hospital care, including the following:
    - Bronchopulmonary dysplasia
    - Cystic fibrosis
    - Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD)
    - Emphysema
    - Asthma
- Chronic conditions such as:
    - Diabetes or other chronic metabolic disorders
    - Liver problems, including cirrhosis
    - Kidney problems
    - Blood disorders, including hemoglobinopathy
    - Cancer
- Medical conditions that may affect the ability to expel respiratory secretions and the ability to swallow, including the following:
    - Cognitive disorders
    - Spinal cord injuries
    - Convulsive disorders
    - Neuromuscular disorders
    - Morbid obesity
    - Down syndrome

## COVID-19 vaccines

Official source: https://www.quebec.ca/en/health/advice-and-prevention/vaccination/covid-19-vaccine

COVID-19 vaccines can be given as of 6 months of age.

They do not protect against colds and respiratory infections caused by other viruses, such as the flu.

#### Number of doses and intervals between doses

The number of doses and the intervals between doses depend on the person’s age, vaccination history and health status. A health professional will be able to tell you the number of doses required for your situation.

A person should wait six months after getting a COVID-19 vaccine before getting a new dose.

Vaccination is safe for people who have had COVID-19 recently.

## Reactions that may occur after vaccination

Official source: https://www.quebec.ca/en/health/advice-and-prevention/vaccination/covid-19-vaccine

The COVID-19 vaccine is safe. Most reactions are mild and short-lived.

Reactions are less common in children and people over 55 years of age. They are more common after the second dose.

Nature and frequency of reactions that may occur after the vaccine
| Frequency | Reactions that may occur after the vaccine |
| --- | --- |
| In most cases
(more than 50% of people) |
- Pain at the injection site

 |
| Very often
(less than 50% of people) |

- Headache, fatigue
- Fever or chills
- Joint pain
- Muscle pain
- Malaise, nausea, vomiting
- Swollen lymph nodes in the armpits
- Redness or swelling at the injection site

 |
| Often
(less than 10% of people) |

- Diarrhea, loss of appetite
- Irritability, drowsiness

 |
| Very rarely (fewer than 1 in 10,000 people) |

- Myocarditis or pericarditis after vaccination\*
- Severe allergic reaction\*\*

 |

\*The risk of myocarditis or pericarditis is lower after doses in subsequent years than after the second dose of the primary series. People who develop myocarditis or pericarditis after vaccination most often have mild symptoms and recover quickly.

\*\*This reaction is more common than is usually expected after a vaccine, but is still very rare.

Other health issues, such as a cold or gastroenteritis, may occur after vaccination, but they are not related to the vaccine.

## What to do after vaccination

Official source: https://www.quebec.ca/en/health/advice-and-prevention/vaccination/covid-19-vaccine

#### Guidelines to follow immediately after vaccination

Wait 15 minutes before leaving the premises where you received the vaccine. If an allergic reaction occurs, the symptoms will appear a few minutes after the vaccination.

If you feel side effects, immediately inform the person who gave you the vaccine. That person will be able to treat you on the spot.

#### Guidelines to follow at home

Use fever or pain medication as needed.

If you experience redness, pain or swelling at the injection site, place a cool damp cloth (cold compress) over the affected area.

#### When to seek medical attention

See a doctor if one of the following situations applies to you:

- You experience serious and unusual symptoms.
- Your symptoms get worse instead of better.
- Your symptoms last over 48 hours.
