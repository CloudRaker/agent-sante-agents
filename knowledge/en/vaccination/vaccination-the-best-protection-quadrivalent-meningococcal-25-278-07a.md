## Vaccination, the best protection

Official source: https://publications.msss.gouv.qc.ca/msss/fichiers/fiches_vaccins/25-278-07A.pdf

By having your child vaccinated, you are protecting him or her against meningococcal infections and their complications. Meningitis (infection of the brain lining) and meningococcemia (infection of the blood) are 2 serious infections caused by meningococcus. There are different serogroups (or types) of meningococcus, among other serogroups A, B, C, W and Y. This vaccine protects against serogroup A, C, W and Y.

|**Disease**|**Signs and symptoms**|**Possible complications**|
|---|---|---|
|**Meningococcal**<br>**infections**|High fever<br>Severe headache<br>Nausea and vomiting<br>General feeling of illness<br>Red marks or tiny pin-size hemorrhages or bruises<br>on the skin|Deafness<br>Scars<br>Permanent brain damage<br>Amputation of hands or feet<br>Death (5 to 15 % of cases even after treatment)|

## **Information on the** Quadrivalent Meningococcal Conjugate Vaccine

Official source: https://publications.msss.gouv.qc.ca/msss/fichiers/fiches_vaccins/25-278-07A.pdf

Vaccination is the protection against meningococcal serogroup A, C, W and Y infections and their complications. This vaccine is usually given in Secondary 3. The vaccine is recommended for persons with a high risk of infection from meningococcus. It is also recommended for persons travelling to regions where the risk of meningococcal infection is high.

**Some symptoms may be caused by the vaccine (e.g. redness at the injection site). Other problems may occur by chance and are not related to the vaccine (e.g. cold, gastro, headache).**

**Meningococcal quadrivalent conjugate vaccine is safe. Most reactions are harmless and do not last long.**

|**Frequency**|**Possible reactions to the vaccine**|**What to do**|
|---|---|---|
|In most cases<br>(more than 50% of people)<br>Very often<br>(less than 50% of people)|Pain at the injection site<br>Redness and swelling at the injection site<br>Headache, muscle pain, fatigue, discomfort, nausea, pain in joints,<br>diarrhea, loss of appetite, irritability, drowsiness (sleepiness)|Apply a cold, damp compress<br>at the injection site.<br>Use a medication for fever<br>or discomfort, if needed.<br>See a health professional|
|Often<br>(less than 10 % of people)|Fever, shivering, vomiting, skin rash|if symptoms are severe.|

We recommend that you stay at the clinic for at least 15 minutes after vaccination because allergic reactions may occur. If an allergic reaction occurs, the symptoms will appear a few minutes after the vaccination. The person giving the vaccine will be able to treat this reaction immediately.

FOR ANY QUESTIONS, contact the person giving the vaccine, or Info-Santé 811 or a health professional.

You can also consult **Québec.ca/immunization**
