## Eligibility (https://www.quebec.ca/en/health/blood-tissue-and-organ-donation/compensation-plan-for-victims-of-a-hema-quebec-product/eligibility)

Official source: https://www.quebec.ca/en/health/blood-tissue-and-organ-donation/compensation-plan-for-victims-of-a-hema-quebec-product/eligibility

To be eligible, individuals must meet the compensation plan's eligibility criteria for victims of a Héma-Québec product.

To be considered a victim, a person must:

- Have suffered bodily injury caused by a product distributed by Héma-Québec, which is defective or contaminated by known or unknown pathogens. Pathogens are infectious agents (e.g., a virus, bacteria or parasite) that cause a disease
- And meet one of the following conditions:
    - Have received a defective product distributed by Héma-Québec after September 27, 1998 during a medical procedure performed in Québec
    - Have contracted the disease from someone who has received a defective product distributed by Héma-Québec after September 27, 1998 in a Québec establishment. Transmission of this disease may have occurred as a result of an injury or sexual contact, for instance
    - Be the child conceived by, and born alive and viable to a person considered a victim according to the previous conditions

> ## Bodily injury
>
> A bodily injury is serious, physical or psychological, permanent damage, death included.
>
> Several **adverse reactions** following the administration of a product does not constitute bodily injury. To know more about this, please see [Regulation respecting the conditions for compensation to victims of a Héma-Québec product](http://legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/ShowDoc/cr/H-1.1,%20r.%201).

### Deadline

To be eligible for the compensation plan, you must submit a compensation claim to the Institut national de santé publique (INSPQ) within 3 years from the date the bodily injury is first manifested.

## Legal framework (https://www.quebec.ca/en/health/blood-tissue-and-organ-donation/compensation-plan-for-victims-of-a-hema-quebec-product/legal-framework)

Official source: https://www.quebec.ca/en/health/blood-tissue-and-organ-donation/compensation-plan-for-victims-of-a-hema-quebec-product/legal-framework

The [Act Respecting Héma-Québec and the Biovigilance Committee](http://legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/ShowDoc/cs/H-1.1) ensures a legal framework for Héma-Québec activities. It includes a chapter (articles 54.1 to 54.12) which provides compensation for victims of a product distributed by Héma-Québec.

The [Regulation respecting the conditions for compensation to victims of a Héma-Québec](http://legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/ShowDoc/cr/H-1.1,%20r.%201) product stipulates elements of the compensation plan, especially eligibility criteria and the terms of a compensation claim.

## Cost (https://www.quebec.ca/en/health/blood-tissue-and-organ-donation/donating-your-body-to-science/cost)

Official source: https://www.quebec.ca/en/health/blood-tissue-and-organ-donation/donating-your-body-to-science/cost

### Transporting the body

The donor’s next-of-kin generally do not have to pay for transportation of the body to the academic institution. These institutions collect bodies for free up to a distance of 250 km (60 km in the case of McGill University). However, when the distance covered to pick up the body is greater, next-of-kin are charged a transportation fee for the additional distance.

### Private funeral arrangements and recovery of the body or ashes

After the study period, institutions proceed with the burial or cremation of the donor’s body. The body or the ashes are buried in a private cemetery plot at no cost to next-of-kin. However, they may wish to conduct a religious ceremony or to have the body buried in a particular place. In such instances, the ceremony and burial are at the next-of-kin’s expense. Some institutions charges for the recovery of ashes.

## Eligibility (https://www.quebec.ca/en/health/blood-tissue-and-organ-donation/donating-your-body-to-science/eligibility)

Official source: https://www.quebec.ca/en/health/blood-tissue-and-organ-donation/donating-your-body-to-science/eligibility

Anyone 14 years of age and over can donate their body to education and research. People under 14 can also donate their bodies with the consent of their parents or guardian ([article 43 of the Civil Code of Quebec](http://legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/ShowDoc/cs/CCQ-1991)).

### Criteria for accepting a body

When a person dies, certain criteria apply in order for their body to be accepted by recognised academic institutions.

**Even when a body meets all acceptance criteria, it is possible that the academic institution refuses it if they have enough cadavers at the time**.

The main acceptance criteria are the following:

- The body must not have been embalmed or undergone an autopsy. Given that the academic institution recovers the body within 48 hours of the death, it cannot be exposed for viewing
- The body must contain all its vital organs; organ donors therefore cannot donate their body (except those who have made a cornea donation)
- The weight of the body should be proportional to its size. The body must be that of an adult, weighing less than 90.7 kg (200 pounds) and less than 1.82 m (6 feet)
- The body must not be deformed. For example, a body with curled members will be refused
- The body must not have been burned or have sustained a major accident
- The deceased must not have jaundice. The death must not have been caused by a contagious disease (Hepatitis A, B or C, HIV, septicemia, septic shock, MRSA, SARS, _Clostridium difficile_, etc.)

## Program Objectives (https://www.quebec.ca/en/health/blood-tissue-and-organ-donation/donating-your-body-to-science/program-objectives)

Official source: https://www.quebec.ca/en/health/blood-tissue-and-organ-donation/donating-your-body-to-science/program-objectives

In Quebec, a person can decide to donate their body to an academic institution so that when they die, it may be used for education or research. Certain criteria apply for a body to be accepted by recognised academic institutions.

Through such donations, college and university students receive practical training in human anatomy through various science and health programs. In these trainings, students practice different discipline-related techniques.

Donations of bodies also enable medical specialists and other health professionals to develop new techniques and procedures for care and intervention.

In Quebec, six academic institutions receive bodies for education or research:

- Université Laval
- McGill University
- Université de Sherbrooke
- Université du Québec à Trois-Rivières
- Collège de Rosemont

Donating your body to science is done anonymously. Only authorised persons have access to donor information. This information is provided for management purposes only. For their part, students are subject to a code of ethics concerning respect of the body.

## About living kidney donation (https://www.quebec.ca/en/health/blood-tissue-and-organ-donation/living-kidney-donation/description)

Official source: https://www.quebec.ca/en/health/blood-tissue-and-organ-donation/living-kidney-donation/description

In Québec, for several years now, a person can decide to donate one of their kidneys to a person with kidney failure during their lifetime. The Québec Living Kidney Donation Program, created in August 2018, aims, among other things, to raise awareness of this possibility and to better support potential donors during the process.

Kidney failure means that the kidneys are unable to filter and remove waste products from blood. It is often treated by dialysis, a replacement therapy that involves passing blood through a membrane to remove accumulated waste. A kidney transplant is another type of replacement therapy for kidney failure.

The kidney can come from a living or a deceased donor. Living kidney donation is, however, preferred since it:

- gives better long-term results in terms of blood filtration efficiency and how long the transplanted kidney lasts;
- significantly reduces the wait time for a transplant;
- can reduce or prevent the need for dialysis treatments before a kidney transplant.

A living donor can be, for example, the recipient’s father, sister or cousin or, alternatively, a loved one who is not directly related to the recipient.

The results of a living donation will be just as good if the compatible donor is a partner, friend or co-worker as if they were a family member. The results will also be better than with a transplant from a deceased donor.

The donor may also not know a recipient and decide to donate a kidney. The kidney is transplanted into a person the donor does not know who is waiting for a transplant.

For more information, see the [My Transplant Coach Canada](https://www.blood.ca/en/organs-tissues/living-organ-donation/my-transplant-coach-canada) tool from the Canadian blood Services.

## Living kidney donor risks (https://www.quebec.ca/en/health/blood-tissue-and-organ-donation/living-kidney-donation/donor-risks)

Official source: https://www.quebec.ca/en/health/blood-tissue-and-organ-donation/living-kidney-donation/donor-risks

If the donor does not have any prior health problems, they can live a normal life with only one kidney. The filtration capacity of the remaining kidney increases and partially compensates for the filtration work of the kidney that was removed.

After donating a kidney, once the donor has recovered, they can resume their activities. They do not have to take medication on a regular basis.

There is a certain risk associated with any surgery. The risk of significant complications in the case of kidney removal is less than 5% and the risk of death is estimated to be 0.03%. Several years after kidney donation, some donors may experience medical complications, such as:

- a slight increase in blood pressure;
- an increase in the amount of protein in urine;
- a deterioration in the filtration efficiency of the remaining kidney, which can lead to kidney failure.

Last update: February 4, 2021

## Eligibility for living kidney donation (https://www.quebec.ca/en/health/blood-tissue-and-organ-donation/living-kidney-donation/eligibility)

Official source: https://www.quebec.ca/en/health/blood-tissue-and-organ-donation/living-kidney-donation/eligibility

Anyone can decide to donate a kidney. However, they must:

- be 18 years of age or older;
- be able to provide free and informed consent;
- meet various criteria with respect to their physical and psychosocial health.

Some health problems are a contraindication to kidney donation:

- serious heart disease;
- active or recent cancer;
- diabetes;
- kidney disease and kidney failure.

**There is no age limit for living kidney donation if there are no medical contraindications.**

Last update: December 19, 2019

## Legal framework for living kidney donation (https://www.quebec.ca/en/health/blood-tissue-and-organ-donation/living-kidney-donation/legal-framework)

Official source: https://www.quebec.ca/en/health/blood-tissue-and-organ-donation/living-kidney-donation/legal-framework

Organ and tissue donation is governed by sections 19, 24 and 25 of the [Civil Code](http://legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/ShowDoc/cs/CCQ-1991). These provisions relate to consent to care not required by a person’s state of health and alienation of a part of a person’s body.

The [Act to facilitate organ and tissue donation](http://www.assnat.qc.ca/en/travaux-parlementaires/projets-loi/projet-loi-125-39-1.html?appelant=MC) regulates administrative measures and procedures for organ and tissue donation.

The [Act respecting labour standards](http://legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/showdoc/cs/N-1.1) protects the donor’s job. An employee has the right to be absent from work for up to 26 weeks over a 12-month period for organ donation.

## Reimbursement of costs for living kidney donation (https://www.quebec.ca/en/health/blood-tissue-and-organ-donation/living-kidney-donation/reimbursement-of-costs)

Official source: https://www.quebec.ca/en/health/blood-tissue-and-organ-donation/living-kidney-donation/reimbursement-of-costs

Being paid or receiving some form of compensation for organ donation is illegal in Canada.

Donating an organ can result in various costs for the donor during the process. A Reimbursement Program for Living Donors has been created by the Government of Québec to reimburse some of the expenses incurred, such as travel, accommodation and meals.

The reimbursement program is administered by Transplant Québec. Anyone in Québec, Canada or outside Canada who wishes to donate an organ to a recipient who lives in Québec and who has a valid Québec health insurance card is eligible for the program.

For more information about the Reimbursement Program for Living Donors, see the [Living Organ Donation](https://www.transplantquebec.ca/en/living-organ-donation) section on the Transplant Québec website.

Last update: February 4, 2021

## Legal framework (https://www.quebec.ca/en/health/blood-tissue-and-organ-donation/organ-and-tissue-donation/legal-framework)

Official source: https://www.quebec.ca/en/health/blood-tissue-and-organ-donation/organ-and-tissue-donation/legal-framework

Organ and tissue donation is governed by sections 43, 44 and 45 of the [Civil Code of Québec](http://legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/showdoc/cs/CCQ-1991), which sets out the obligations surrounding the expression of consent and certain procedures associated with organ and tissue donation.

The _Civil Code of Québec_ states, for instance, that the donation process cannot start before the death of the donor is attested by two doctors who do not participate either in the removal or in the transplantation. In addition, it obliges the family to follow the deceased person’s wishes. It also prohibits the sale or purchase of human organs or tissues.

Section 204.1 of the [Act respecting health services and social services](http://legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/fr/showdoc/cs/S-4.2) also provides a framework for organ and tissue donation for health professionals in accordance with an established procedure.

The [Act respecting Héma-Québec and the Biovigilance Committee](http://legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/ShowDoc/cs/H-1.1) provides a legal framework for Héma-Québec’s activities, including its mandate relating to human tissue.

Transplant Québec provides a link between the allocation of donor organs and the transplantation needs of recipients by coordinating the management of the single waiting list.

Last update: February 23, 2023

## Adapted Tricycles and Bicycles Program (https://www.quebec.ca/en/health/health-system-and-services/assistive-devices-disabilities-and-handicaps/adapted-tricycles-and-bicycles-program)

Official source: https://www.quebec.ca/en/health/health-system-and-services/assistive-devices-disabilities-and-handicaps/adapted-tricycles-and-bicycles-program

### Description

This program allows for the allocation of tricycles and bicycles suitable for people age 18 and under with disabilities.

### Presentation and objectives

Allocating tricycles and bicycles adapted to minors with disabilities helps promote their social integration, skills development, and autonomy.

Before allocating an adapted tricycle or modifying a tricycle or a bicycle, an overall assessment of the person’s needs and resources must be carried out.

An adapted tricycle or bicycle is a device specially designed to meet the needs of the user.

### Eligibility

To be eligible for this program, you must:

- Have a physical or intellectual impairments causing persistent and significant disabilities;
- Be a minor;
- Be unable to use a standard tricycle or bicycle;
- Be able to pedal and steer the adapted tricycle or bicycle on your own.

You are not eligible if you:

- Already benefit from the same type of assistance provided by another national or federal program or a program offered by private insurers;
- Live in a residential long-term care centre or a public intermediate resource.

### Reimbursable expenses

Technical assistance funded under this program is provided to the user. However, a lump sum of $150 must be paid by the parent for the purchase of a tricycle, an adapted bike, or a handbike. For individuals with private insurance coverage, this program can be used to pay the uncovered portion of the costs.

Allocated equipment must be insured. It must be returned when the person is no longer using it or if he or she dies.

### Process

To enrol in this program, contact your territorial establishment. For contact information, consult the following page: [Find Your Health and Social Services Facility](https://www.sante.gouv.qc.ca/en/repertoire-ressources/votre-cisss-ciusss/).

You will need to provide a medical certificate relating to the impairment.

### Help and information

For assistance, contact the [Office des personnes handicapées du Québec](https://www.quebec.ca/gouvernement/ministeres-et-organismes/office-personnes-handicapees/coordonnees).

## Bandages and Compression Garments Used to Treat Lymphedema (https://www.quebec.ca/en/health/health-system-and-services/assistive-devices-disabilities-and-handicaps/bandages-and-compression-garments-used-to-treat-lymphedema)

Official source: https://www.quebec.ca/en/health/health-system-and-services/assistive-devices-disabilities-and-handicaps/bandages-and-compression-garments-used-to-treat-lymphedema

This program is intended for persons with primary or secondary [lymphedema](https://www.quebec.ca/en/health/health-issues/a-z/lymphedema). The program provides for the reimbursement, in part or in full, of the cost of bandages and compression garments.

Covered supplies:

## Program for radical suburethral sling removal surgery services provided outside Québec (https://www.quebec.ca/en/health/health-system-and-services/insurance-schemes/program-for-radical-suburethral-sling-removal-surgery-services-provided-outside-quebec)

Official source: https://www.quebec.ca/en/health/health-system-and-services/insurance-schemes/program-for-radical-suburethral-sling-removal-surgery-services-provided-outside-quebec

Under the program for radical suburethral sling removal surgery services provided outside Québec, a portion of the cost of the surgery is reimbursed.

The aim of the program is to provide financial support to women who had complications following the placement of their suburethral sling and who had to pay for a surgical procedure to have their sling removed outside Québec.

### Eligibility

All women insured by the Québec public health insurance plan who underwent suburethral sling removal surgery between October 1, 2018 and December 31, 2020 in a hospital centre located outside Québec are eligible for the program. Due to the current context of the COVID-19 health crisis, women who had to use this service outside Québec in January and February 2021 may also be reimbursed.

Women who would like to undergo removal surgery must consult their family physician to find out what to do.

### Reimbursement of costs

The program provides $22,500 in financial assistance to cover the medical and hospital services required for the surgery. For details of the eligibility conditions and the terms and conditions of the program, visit the [Radical suburethral sling removal surgery](http://www.ramq.gouv.qc.ca/en/citizens/aid-programs/surgery-radical-removal-suburethral-sling) page on the website of the Régie de l'assurance maladie du Québec (RAMQ).

### Procedure

The RAMQ manages the program for the Ministère de la Santé et des Services sociaux (MSSS).

Go to the [Radical suburethral sling removal surgery](http://www.ramq.gouv.qc.ca/en/citizens/aid-programs/surgery-radical-removal-suburethral-sling) page for instructions on how to submit your claim and download the forms.

## Attestation of a request for health services or social services for a child without the other parent’s consent (https://www.quebec.ca/en/health/health-system-and-services/requesting-services-for-your-child-without-other-parent-consent/attestation)

Official source: https://www.quebec.ca/en/health/health-system-and-services/requesting-services-for-your-child-without-other-parent-consent/attestation

This attestation allows you to request health services (including health care) or social services for your child without the other parent’s consent, due to a situation of sexual, spousal or family violence.

The Minister of Justice designates individuals to issue the attestation, who must be criminal and penal prosecuting attorneys working for the Directeur des poursuites criminelles et pénales, acting as public officers.

These public officers must attest that the services will benefit the child’s health and safety.

[Download attestation](https://cdn-contenu.quebec.ca/cdn-contenu/adm/org/dpcp/PDF/demande_services_sante_sans_consentement_dun_parent/FO_ENG_demande_attestation_services_sante_services_sociaux_en_ligne_DPCP.pdf)

### Grounds for requesting an attestation

Both parents have parental authority. Both must therefore consent to the services provided for their child.

Although the parents exercise parental authority jointly, a parent acting alone may consent to services and is presumed to be acting the consent of the other parent.

- You can request an attestation if, as a result of a violent situation:
- you want your child to receive services and the other parent refuses;

it is not safe for you to ask for the other parent's consent.

### Scope of the attestation

The attestation entitles the holder to obtain only the services designated by the Minister.

This is an **exceptional measure**. It does not replace the law governing the care required by a child's state of health, nor the powers of the Director of Youth Protection.
