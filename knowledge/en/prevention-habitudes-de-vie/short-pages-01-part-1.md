## Tooth brushing in children age 6 and under with fluoride toothpaste - Parent's guide (https://publications.msss.gouv.qc.ca/msss/fichiers/2022/22-231-08A.pdf)

Official source: https://publications.msss.gouv.qc.ca/msss/fichiers/2022/22-231-08A.pdf

**1.** Brush your child’s teeth yourself as a first rule. If your child wants to do it on their own, that’s fine, but you should still complete the brushing afterwards.

**2.** Brushing should be at least twice a day.

**3.** Brush for two minutes each time.

**4.** Use fluoride toothpaste.

**5.** Encourage your child to spit out any excess toothpaste.

**6.** Ask your child not to rinse their mouth after brushing. That way, fluoride in the toothpaste will continue to protect the teeth against decay.

**7.** Floss (or use interdental toothpick brushes) once a day if your child’s teeth touch each other.

**8.** Have your child’s teeth checked by your dentist at least once a year.

**The first teeth** are important to help your child chew and speak properly. They also help make sure the adult teeth will have the room they need.

TOOTH BRUSHING IN CHILDREN AGE 6 AND UNDER WITH FLUORIDE TOOTHPASTE

### Which type of toothpaste should you use?
You should always use **toothpaste with fluoride** : the fluoride in toothpaste makes teeth stronger and reduces the action of bacteria that cause tooth decay.

Keep toothpaste in a place that is out of the reach of children.

### How much fluoride toothpaste should you use?
For children **under 3 years of age** , use an amount the size of **a grain of rice** .

For children **age 3 to 6** , **a pea-sized** amount should be used.

### When should you brush your child’s teeth?
To protect the teeth fully from tooth decay, brush your child’s teeth **at least twice a day for two minutes.** Ideally, after every meal and before going to bed!

2<br>twice<br>MINUTES<br>at least<br>a day<br>

Brushing your teeth **with fluoride toothpaste** prevents tooth decay.

### Which type of toothbrush should you use?
You should use a toothbrush that is **appropriate for your child’s age** . It is important to use a brush that has:

- soft bristles, to avoid hurting the gums;

- a small round head, to reach all the teeth;

To help you choose a toothbrush, you can also look at the age indicated on the packaging.

### When should you change toothbrush?
You should change toothbrush **every three months** or **when the bristles have become bent or flattened** .

## Oral and Dental Services in Pediatric Oncology Program (https://publications.msss.gouv.qc.ca/msss/fichiers/2025/25-902-02WA.pdf)

Official source: https://publications.msss.gouv.qc.ca/msss/fichiers/2025/25-902-02WA.pdf

**Oral and Dental Services in Pediatric Oncology Program**

**A healthy mouth is important. We’re here to help.**

### Did you know?
**Cancer therapy can weaken the immune system and increase the risks associated with dental infections. Cancer and cancer therapy can also affect teeth and jaw development.**

In order to prevent, limit and treat dental complications and the consequences associated with cancer or cancer therapy, the Oral and Dental Services in Pediatric Oncology Program provides free access to dental care that is not covered by the Régie de l’assurance maladie du Québec (RAMQ).

### Who is eligible?
**The program is intended for all patients under 18 years of age who have been diagnosed with cancer or who require a bone marrow transplant, and who meet at least one of the following criteria:**

- They need oral and dental services before starting therapy;

- They need preventive oral and dental services during therapy;

- They are at risk of developing oral and dental consequences associated with cancer or cancer therapy;

- They have oral and dental consequences associated with cancer or cancer therapy. The consequences must be caused directly by the cancer or the cancer therapy received (e.g., surgery, radiation therapy, chemotherapy).

In order to access the program, the patient must live permanently in Quebec and have a valid RAMQ card.

To be covered, the dental care required during the cancer therapy period must be provided by one of the five designated centres: **CHU Sainte-Justine CHU de Sherbrooke CHU de Québec Montreal Children’s Hospital CHEO**

Eligibility for coverage of treatments for oral and dental consequences associated with cancer will be assessed by the people in charge of the program following a referral by a health professional.

#### Take care of your dental health during treatment
During the treatment period, your child may no longer like the taste or texture of some foods – for example, your child might refuse to eat meals they liked before, toothpaste might seem to taste stronger, and the texture of the toothbrush might feel unpleasant. These concerns add to everything you have to think about on a daily basis. For support, do not hesitate to talk to your health care team.

## Toothache (https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/toothache/)

Official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/toothache/

Oral and dental pain

### What it is

A toothache is pain in one or more teeth, the gums, or the mouth. It can be caused by a cavity, an infection, or an injury. A toothache can make it hard to eat or sleep.

### What you can do at home

What to do

- Eat soft foods, such as purées, fruit compote, yogurt, pudding, or shredded or ground meat.
- Serve lukewarm or cold food, if needed.
- Gently brush the teeth with a soft toothbrush after every meal or snack.
- Floss once a day.
- To reduce pain, take ibuprofen (Advil). When in doubt, ask a pharmacist for advice.

Important: Ibuprofen is contraindicated for children under 6 months of age.

**Apply a cold compress to the cheek**

- Cold helps reduce pain.
- Apply cold for 15 to 20 minutes every 2 to 3 hours.
- Use a cold compress, such as, an ice pack, a bag of frozen vegetables, or a cold gel pack.
- Always place a cloth between the skin and the cold source to protect the skin.

Avoid

- Avoid smoking

### When to seek medical attention

Toothache accompanied by a fever

Pain that lasts more than 2 days or becomes very severe

Toothache with a headache or earache

Pus (yellow fluid) coming out of the gums

Gums that bleed often

A red or white patch in the mouth that is painful, swollen, or bleeds easily

Need help?

See your dentist.

Call Info-Santé at 811, Option 1, to speak with a nurse at any time.

Go to the pharmacy for advice on medications or treatment.

Make an appointment at your medical clinic or CLSC.

If you do not have a family doctor, go to [gap.soinsvirtuels](https://gap.soinsvirtuels.gouv.qc.ca/en) or call 811, Option 3.

### When to go to the hospital right away

Unable to open or close the mouth

Jaw pain, headache, and difficulty seeing clearly

Pain in the jaw and neck, chest, shoulder, or arm

Toothache with fever and swelling (edema) of the cheek, or of the base of the nose and an eyelid

Fever in people at higher risk:

- weakened immune system (immunocompromised)
- pregnancy
- babies younger than 3 months of age

Pain that continues or gets worse, even after following this advice

## Recovery of used syringes and needles (https://www.quebec.ca/en/health/advice-and-prevention/accidents-injuries-and-diseases-prevention/recovery-of-used-syringes-and-needles)

Official source: https://www.quebec.ca/en/health/advice-and-prevention/accidents-injuries-and-diseases-prevention/recovery-of-used-syringes-and-needles

### Integrated Used Syringe and Needle Recovery System (SIRSAU)

The integrated used syringe and needle recovery system (SIRSAU) aims to securely collect needles and syringes that have been used by:

- People who take certain medical treatments at home, such as diabetes injections
- People who inject drugs

These people can get a free container for safe disposal (sharps disposal container) by simply requesting one in any of the following places:

- Community pharmacies
- [Injection Equipment Access Centers (CAMIs)](https://www.quebec.ca/en/health/advice-and-prevention/alcohol-drugs-gambling/distribution-of-new-injection-equipment#c417) located in some, but not all, community organizations, CLSCs and hospitals
- Outpatient diabetes clinics and diabetes education centres located in CLSCs or hospitals

Public or private health care facilities, such as CHSLDs and private seniors’ residences, must provide, at their expense, sharps disposal containers for their residents.

People who have received sharps disposal containers through SIRSAU can return them to:

- Community pharmacies
- CLSCs
- Hospitals
- Community organizations that distribute new injection equipment

These organizations take back the containers free of charge.

SIRSAU is in place throughout all off Québec. The used syringe and needle recovery system comes in addition to other existing services, including:

- The collection of biomedical waste by CLSCs and hospitals.
- The installation of syringe collection boxes in outdoor public places.
- The collection of used needles and syringes by volunteers.

## Used syringes: what to do to prevent accidents and the transmission of infections (https://www.quebec.ca/en/health/advice-and-prevention/accidents-injuries-and-diseases-prevention/used-syringes-prevent-accidents-and-infections)

Official source: https://www.quebec.ca/en/health/advice-and-prevention/accidents-injuries-and-diseases-prevention/used-syringes-prevent-accidents-and-infections

If you find a used syringe, you must take precautions to prevent accidents and the possible transmission of infections. You can pick the syringe up safely yourself or contact [Info-Santé 811](https://www.quebec.ca/en/health/finding-a-resource/info-sante-811) to arrange for it to be picked up.

Teach your children that they should never touch a syringe. Tell them to let an adult know right away if they find one.

### How to pick a syringe up safely

If you find a syringe, pick it up safely by following these steps. Be careful because there might be other syringes nearby.

#### Step 1

Get a thick, hard plastic container with a lid that closes. For example, you can use an empty peanut butter jar or a yogurt or margarine container.

Do not use glass containers because they could break.

Choose a container with a fairly large opening because you could injure yourself if the opening is too small.

Open the container and place it near the syringe.

#### Step 2

Pick up the syringe carefully by the blunt end so that the needle is pointing away from your body.

Even if you find the syringe cap, do not try to put it back on the needle because you could injure yourself.

Place the syringe into the container.

#### Step 3

Close the lid tightly.

#### Step 4

Wash your hands thoroughly with soap and water.

#### Step 5

**Take the container to a CLSC or pharmacy.**

**Never throw a container with a syringe in it in a recycling bin or garbage can because** other people could injure themselves.

**If you cannot find a container** to put the syringe in and transport it safely, **do not touch it and call [Info-Santé 811](https://www.quebec.ca/en/health/finding-a-resource/info-sante-811)**.

### If you accidentally injure yourself

If you injure yourself with a used syringe:

- Clean the wound with soap and water.
- See a doctor as soon as possible, preferably within 2 hours. For example, you can go to an emergency room. As soon as you get there, tell the staff that you have injured yourself with a used syringe.

## Alcochoix+ (https://www.quebec.ca/en/health/advice-and-prevention/alcohol-drugs-gambling/alcochoix-plus)

Official source: https://www.quebec.ca/en/health/advice-and-prevention/alcohol-drugs-gambling/alcochoix-plus

### Description

Alcochoix+ is a program that empowers people to make decisions regarding alcohol consumption. It helps them choose where, when, with whom and how much they drink. Alcochoix+ is for people who:

- Are concerned about the consequences of their drinking habits
- Want to change their drinking habits

Alcochoix+ places you front and centre; you are the expert on what is best for you. The program aims to support you in your efforts and help you find solutions. Each move to reduce risks associated with alcohol consumption is a step in the right direction. Every step in that direction deserves encouragement.

### Procedure

Alcochoix+ offers 3 options to suit different needs:

- **Self-directed option:** You follow the program on your own, using the Alcochoix+ guide. You progress at your own pace over a period of 6 to 8 weeks.
- **Guided option:** In addition to using the Alcochoix+ guide, you meet with an Alcochoix+ counsellor a few times. The counsellor answers your questions, guides you and supports you in your efforts.
- **Group option:** You attend 6 group sessions. During each 90-minute session, an Alcochoix+ counsellor outlines key elements of the program. You can also discuss with other participants about hurdles encountered, strategies that work for you, your progress, etc.

The program is offered at CLSCs. Options available may differ from one CLSC to another.

### Participating in Alcochoix+

You can participate in the program if:

- You are 18 years old and over
- You are concerned about your drinking
- You simply want to learn more about Alcochoix+

Find out more at your local CLSC. You can talk to a health-care professional; **it is free and confidential**. The health-care professional will assess whether Alcochoix+ suits your needs and personal situation.

To find contact info for your local CLSC, go to [Finding a CLSC](https://sante.gouv.qc.ca/en/repertoire-ressources/clsc/).

## Recognizing a risk behaviour associated with gambling (https://www.quebec.ca/en/health/advice-and-prevention/alcohol-drugs-gambling/recognizing-a-gambling-disorder)

Official source: https://www.quebec.ca/en/health/advice-and-prevention/alcohol-drugs-gambling/recognizing-a-gambling-disorder

Playing the lottery, bingo, video lottery terminals and card games with friends are all forms of entertainment. Gambling, however, can involve risks that can affect different aspects of a person's life or lead to a gambling disorder.

Here are  examples that allow recognizing behavior:

- Constantly thinking about gambling
- Needing to gamble with increasingly large sums of money to reach the desired state of excitement
- Having difficulty stopping gambling, gambling less or setting limits when gambling despite a genuine desire and effort to do so
- Feeling restless or irritable when trying to gamble less or stop gambling
- Gambling to escape difficulties or to relieve an emotional state such as anxiety
- Continuing to gamble to "get back your losses"
- Lying to family and friends to hide from them how important and invasive gambling habits have become for your well-being
- Committing illegal acts to finance gambling
- Jeopardizing your studies, work or personal relationships
- Relying on others to get out of difficult financial situations caused by gambling

### What to do

If you think that you have one or several of these risk behaviours or if you are worried about someone you know who seems to be adopting such behaviours, there are ways you can protect yourself. See the page [Reducing the risk of developing disorders associated with drinking, using drugs and gambling](https://www.quebec.ca/en/health/advice-and-prevention/alcohol-drugs-gambling/reducing-the-risk-of-developping-disorders).

### Help and resources

You know, you can get help and information by consulting the following resources:

- Your [territorial establishment](https://sante.gouv.qc.ca/en/repertoire-ressources/votre-cisss-ciusss/);
- [Gambling – Help and Referral](https://aidejeu.ca/en/): [1‑800‑461‑0140](tel:+18004610140)

## The role of luck in gambling (https://www.quebec.ca/en/health/advice-and-prevention/alcohol-drugs-gambling/the-role-of-luck-in-gambling)

Official source: https://www.quebec.ca/en/health/advice-and-prevention/alcohol-drugs-gambling/the-role-of-luck-in-gambling

### Online gambling

It is generally known that the odds of winning are lower on fee-based websites than on those where people can play without betting money.

### Poker

In games that require skill, such as table or online poker, it is the combination of a person’s skill and luck that determines the outcome. When two players are equally skilled, chance determines the winner.

### Other gambling activities

There is **no way** to predict the outcome of **any** gambling activity:

- Slot machines
- Video lottery terminals
- Casino games or gaming rooms
- Sporting bets
- Bingo
- Lotteries, etc.
